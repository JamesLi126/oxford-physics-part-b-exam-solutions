\section*{Question 1 --- Affine connection, covariant derivative, Riemann tensor}

\textbf{Problem.} (a) Define $\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}$ via locally free-falling coordinates and write $V^\mu_{;\nu}$. (b) Show $\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}$ is not a tensor. (c) Show $V^\mu_{;\sigma;\tau}-V^\mu_{;\tau;\sigma}=R^\mu_{\;\lambda\sigma\tau}V^\lambda$. (d) Argue $R^\mu_{\;\lambda\sigma\tau}$ is therefore a tensor. (e) Relations between $R^\mu_{\;\lambda\sigma\tau}$, $R_{\lambda\mu\sigma\tau}$, $R_{\mu\nu}$, $R$; symmetries of $R_{\lambda\mu\sigma\tau}$.

\subsection*{(a) Affine connection from local inertial frame}

Erect locally free-falling (LIF) coordinates $\xi^\alpha$ at event $P$. Free particles obey:
\[ \frac{\de^2 \xi^\alpha}{\de\tau^2} = 0. \]
Apply the chain rule to $\xi^\alpha(x^\mu)$:
\[ \frac{\de \xi^\alpha}{\de\tau} = \pd{\xi^\alpha}{x^\mu}\frac{\de x^\mu}{\de\tau}. \]
Differentiate once more using the product rule:
\[ \frac{\de^2 \xi^\alpha}{\de\tau^2} = \pd{\xi^\alpha}{x^\mu}\frac{\de^2 x^\mu}{\de\tau^2} + \frac{\partial^2 \xi^\alpha}{\partial x^\mu \partial x^\nu}\frac{\de x^\mu}{\de\tau}\frac{\de x^\nu}{\de\tau}. \]
Set the left side to zero:
\[ \pd{\xi^\alpha}{x^\mu}\frac{\de^2 x^\mu}{\de\tau^2} + \frac{\partial^2 \xi^\alpha}{\partial x^\mu \partial x^\nu}\frac{\de x^\mu}{\de\tau}\frac{\de x^\nu}{\de\tau} = 0. \]
Multiply through by $\partial x^\lambda/\partial \xi^\alpha$ and use $(\partial x^\lambda/\partial\xi^\alpha)(\partial\xi^\alpha/\partial x^\mu) = \delta^\lambda_\mu$:
\[ \frac{\de^2 x^\lambda}{\de\tau^2} + \pd{x^\lambda}{\xi^\alpha}\frac{\partial^2 \xi^\alpha}{\partial x^\mu\partial x^\nu}\frac{\de x^\mu}{\de\tau}\frac{\de x^\nu}{\de\tau} = 0. \]
Define the affine connection:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu} \equiv \pd{x^\lambda}{\xi^\alpha}\,\frac{\partial^2 \xi^\alpha}{\partial x^\mu\,\partial x^\nu}.\;}
\label{eq:gamma-def}
\end{equation}
Geodesic equation becomes $\ddot x^\lambda + \Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu \dot x^\nu = 0$.

Covariant derivative of a vector $V^\mu$:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;V^\mu_{\;;\nu} = \pd{V^\mu}{x^\nu} + \Gamma^\mu_{\lambda\nu} V^\lambda.\;}
\label{eq:covderiv}
\end{equation}
The added $\Gamma$-term cancels the non-tensorial piece of $\partial_\nu V^\mu$ so that $V^\mu_{\;;\nu}$ transforms as a $(1,1)$ tensor.

\subsection*{(b) $\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}$ is not a tensor}

Keep the LIF $\xi^\alpha$ but change chart $x^\mu \to x'^{\mu}$. Apply definition \eqref{eq:gamma-def} in primed coordinates:
\[ \Gamma'^\lambda_{\mu\nu} = \pd{x'^\lambda}{\xi^\alpha}\,\frac{\partial^2 \xi^\alpha}{\partial x'^\mu \partial x'^\nu}. \]
Expand the first derivative by chain rule:
\[ \pd{\xi^\alpha}{x'^\nu} = \pd{x^\rho}{x'^\nu}\pd{\xi^\alpha}{x^\rho}. \]
Differentiate w.r.t.\ $x'^\mu$ using the product rule:
\[ \frac{\partial^2 \xi^\alpha}{\partial x'^\mu \partial x'^\nu} = \pd{x^\sigma}{x'^\mu}\pd{x^\rho}{x'^\nu}\frac{\partial^2 \xi^\alpha}{\partial x^\sigma\partial x^\rho} + \frac{\partial^2 x^\rho}{\partial x'^\mu \partial x'^\nu}\pd{\xi^\alpha}{x^\rho}. \]
Contract with $\partial x'^\lambda/\partial\xi^\alpha$ and apply the chain rule on the inhomogeneous piece:
\[ \pd{x'^\lambda}{\xi^\alpha}\pd{\xi^\alpha}{x^\rho} = \pd{x'^\lambda}{x^\rho}. \]
Insert an intermediate $\partial x^\kappa/\partial\xi^\alpha\cdot\partial x'^\lambda/\partial x^\kappa$ in the homogeneous piece to recognise $\Gamma^\kappa_{\sigma\rho}$:
\[ \pd{x'^\lambda}{\xi^\alpha}\frac{\partial^2\xi^\alpha}{\partial x^\sigma\partial x^\rho} = \pd{x'^\lambda}{x^\kappa}\,\pd{x^\kappa}{\xi^\alpha}\frac{\partial^2\xi^\alpha}{\partial x^\sigma\partial x^\rho} = \pd{x'^\lambda}{x^\kappa}\,\Gamma^\kappa_{\sigma\rho}. \]
Combine:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\Gamma'^\lambda_{\mu\nu} = \pd{x'^\lambda}{x^\kappa}\pd{x^\sigma}{x'^\mu}\pd{x^\rho}{x'^\nu}\,\Gamma^\kappa_{\sigma\rho} + \pd{x'^\lambda}{x^\rho}\frac{\partial^2 x^\rho}{\partial x'^\mu\partial x'^\nu}.\;}
\label{eq:gamma-transf}
\end{equation}
First term is the tensor transformation; the second (inhomogeneous) piece is non-zero whenever the coordinate transformation is non-linear, so $\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}$ is \textbf{not a tensor}.

\subsection*{(c) Commutator of covariant derivatives}

Apply \eqref{eq:covderiv} once on $V^\mu$:
\[ V^\mu_{\;;\sigma} = \partial_\sigma V^\mu + \Gamma^\mu_{\lambda\sigma} V^\lambda. \]
Treat $V^\mu_{\;;\sigma}$ as a $(1,1)$ tensor and apply the rule on covariant derivatives of mixed tensors:
\[ V^\mu_{\;;\sigma;\tau} = \partial_\tau V^\mu_{\;;\sigma} + \Gamma^\mu_{\nu\tau} V^\nu_{\;;\sigma} - \Gamma^\nu_{\sigma\tau} V^\mu_{\;;\nu}. \]
Substitute $V^\mu_{\;;\sigma} = \partial_\sigma V^\mu + \Gamma^\mu_{\lambda\sigma}V^\lambda$:
\[ V^\mu_{\;;\sigma;\tau} = \partial_\tau\bigl(\partial_\sigma V^\mu + \Gamma^\mu_{\lambda\sigma}V^\lambda\bigr) + \Gamma^\mu_{\nu\tau}\bigl(\partial_\sigma V^\nu + \Gamma^\nu_{\lambda\sigma}V^\lambda\bigr) - \Gamma^\nu_{\sigma\tau}\bigl(\partial_\nu V^\mu + \Gamma^\mu_{\lambda\nu}V^\lambda\bigr). \]
Expand the first parenthesis via the product rule:
\[ V^\mu_{\;;\sigma;\tau} = \partial_\tau\partial_\sigma V^\mu + (\partial_\tau \Gamma^\mu_{\lambda\sigma}) V^\lambda + \Gamma^\mu_{\lambda\sigma}\partial_\tau V^\lambda + \Gamma^\mu_{\nu\tau}(\partial_\sigma V^\nu + \Gamma^\nu_{\lambda\sigma} V^\lambda) - \Gamma^\nu_{\sigma\tau}(\partial_\nu V^\mu + \Gamma^\mu_{\lambda\nu}V^\lambda). \]
Swap $\sigma \leftrightarrow \tau$ to obtain the partner:
\[ V^\mu_{\;;\tau;\sigma} = \partial_\sigma\partial_\tau V^\mu + (\partial_\sigma \Gamma^\mu_{\lambda\tau}) V^\lambda + \Gamma^\mu_{\lambda\tau}\partial_\sigma V^\lambda + \Gamma^\mu_{\nu\sigma}(\partial_\tau V^\nu + \Gamma^\nu_{\lambda\tau} V^\lambda) - \Gamma^\nu_{\tau\sigma}(\partial_\nu V^\mu + \Gamma^\mu_{\lambda\nu}V^\lambda). \]
Commute the second partials so they cancel in the difference:
\[ \partial_\tau\partial_\sigma V^\mu - \partial_\sigma\partial_\tau V^\mu = 0. \]
Apply torsion-free symmetry $\Gamma^\nu_{\sigma\tau}=\Gamma^\nu_{\tau\sigma}$ to kill the last bracket of each line:
\[ \Gamma^\nu_{\sigma\tau}(\partial_\nu V^\mu+\Gamma^\mu_{\lambda\nu}V^\lambda) - \Gamma^\nu_{\tau\sigma}(\partial_\nu V^\mu+\Gamma^\mu_{\lambda\nu}V^\lambda) = 0. \]
Collect the $\Gamma\cdot\partial V$ cross-terms from the two expressions:
\[ \bigl(\Gamma^\mu_{\lambda\sigma}\partial_\tau V^\lambda + \Gamma^\mu_{\nu\tau}\partial_\sigma V^\nu\bigr) - \bigl(\Gamma^\mu_{\lambda\tau}\partial_\sigma V^\lambda + \Gamma^\mu_{\nu\sigma}\partial_\tau V^\nu\bigr). \]
Relabel dummies $\nu\to\lambda$ in both inner brackets:
\[ \Gamma^\mu_{\lambda\sigma}\partial_\tau V^\lambda + \Gamma^\mu_{\lambda\tau}\partial_\sigma V^\lambda - \Gamma^\mu_{\lambda\tau}\partial_\sigma V^\lambda - \Gamma^\mu_{\lambda\sigma}\partial_\tau V^\lambda = 0. \]
Only the $\partial\Gamma$ and $\Gamma\Gamma$ brackets remain:
\[ V^\mu_{\;;\sigma;\tau} - V^\mu_{\;;\tau;\sigma} = \bigl[\partial_\tau \Gamma^\mu_{\lambda\sigma} - \partial_\sigma \Gamma^\mu_{\lambda\tau} + \Gamma^\mu_{\nu\tau}\Gamma^\nu_{\lambda\sigma} - \Gamma^\mu_{\nu\sigma}\Gamma^\nu_{\lambda\tau}\bigr] V^\lambda. \]
Identify the bracket with the Riemann tensor (formula on the cover sheet):
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;V^\mu_{\;;\sigma;\tau} - V^\mu_{\;;\tau;\sigma} = R^\mu_{\;\lambda\sigma\tau}\,V^\lambda.\;}
\label{eq:commutator}
\end{equation}

\subsection*{(d) $R^\mu_{\;\lambda\sigma\tau}$ is a tensor}

LHS of \eqref{eq:commutator} is a $(1,3)$ tensor: each covariant derivative produces a tensor, and a difference of tensors is a tensor. RHS is the contraction of $R^\mu_{\;\lambda\sigma\tau}$ with the arbitrary vector $V^\lambda$. By the \textbf{quotient theorem}, since $R^\mu_{\;\lambda\sigma\tau}V^\lambda$ is a tensor for every $V^\lambda$, $R^\mu_{\;\lambda\sigma\tau}$ itself is a $(1,3)$ tensor. The non-tensorial inhomogeneous pieces in the individual $\partial\Gamma$ and $\Gamma\Gamma$ terms cancel in the antisymmetric combination.

\subsection*{(e) Index relations and symmetries}

Lower the upper index with $g_{\mu\lambda}$:
\[ R_{\mu\lambda\sigma\tau} = g_{\mu\nu} R^\nu_{\;\lambda\sigma\tau}. \]
Ricci tensor (contract first upper with last lower, per cover-sheet convention):
\[ R_{\mu\nu} \equiv R^\lambda_{\;\mu\nu\lambda} = g^{\lambda\sigma} R_{\sigma\mu\nu\lambda}. \]
Ricci scalar:
\[ R \equiv g^{\mu\nu} R_{\mu\nu}. \]
Symmetries of $R_{\lambda\mu\sigma\tau}$:
\[ \boxed{\;R_{\lambda\mu\sigma\tau} = -R_{\mu\lambda\sigma\tau} = -R_{\lambda\mu\tau\sigma} = R_{\sigma\tau\lambda\mu},\qquad R_{\lambda[\mu\sigma\tau]}=0,\qquad R_{\lambda\mu[\sigma\tau;\rho]}=0.\;} \]
The first three are the algebraic (anti)symmetries; the cyclic identity (first Bianchi) and the differential (second Bianchi) identities complete the set, reducing the $4^4=256$ components to $20$ in 4D.

\section*{Question 2 --- Rindler frame, Newtonian limit, time dilation, proper acceleration}

\textbf{Problem.} (a) Convert Minkowski to Rindler line element. (b) Newtonian limit. (c) Proper-time ratio at $z'=0$ vs $z'=h$ giving $1+gh/c^2$. (d) Equivalence with gravitational time dilation. (e) Compute invariant 4-acceleration $a$ and show it differs at $z'=0$ and $z'=h$.

\subsection*{(a) Rindler line element}

Differentiate $ct$ in the given transformation:
\[ c\,\de t = \left(\tfrac{c^2}{g} + z'\right)\frac{g}{c}\cosh(gt'/c)\,\de t' + \sinh(gt'/c)\,\de z'. \]
Factor $c^2/g$ out of the bracket:
\[ c\,\de t = \left(1 + \tfrac{gz'}{c^2}\right) c\cosh(gt'/c)\,\de t' + \sinh(gt'/c)\,\de z'. \]
Differentiate $z$ analogously:
\[ \de z = \left(1 + \tfrac{gz'}{c^2}\right) c\sinh(gt'/c)\,\de t' + \cosh(gt'/c)\,\de z'. \]
Square $c\,\de t$ and $\de z$ separately:
\[ c^2\de t^2 = \left(1+\tfrac{gz'}{c^2}\right)^{\!2}c^2\cosh^2\!\de t'^2 + 2\left(1+\tfrac{gz'}{c^2}\right)c\cosh\sinh\,\de t'\de z' + \sinh^2\!\de z'^2, \]
\[ \de z^2 = \left(1+\tfrac{gz'}{c^2}\right)^{\!2}c^2\sinh^2\!\de t'^2 + 2\left(1+\tfrac{gz'}{c^2}\right)c\sinh\cosh\,\de t'\de z' + \cosh^2\!\de z'^2. \]
Subtract; the cross-terms cancel and combine the $\de t'^2,\de z'^2$ coefficients:
\[ -c^2\de t^2 + \de z^2 = -\left(1+\tfrac{gz'}{c^2}\right)^{\!2}c^2\bigl(\cosh^2-\sinh^2\bigr)\de t'^2 + \bigl(\cosh^2-\sinh^2\bigr)\de z'^2. \]
Apply $\cosh^2-\sinh^2=1$:
\[ -c^2\de t^2 + \de z^2 = -\left(1+\tfrac{gz'}{c^2}\right)^{\!2} c^2\,\de t'^2 + \de z'^2. \]
Add the unchanged $\de x^2 + \de y^2 = \de x'^2+\de y'^2$:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\de s^2 = -\left(1+\tfrac{gz'}{c^2}\right)^{\!2}c^2\,\de t'^2 + \de x'^2 + \de y'^2 + \de z'^2.\;}
\label{eq:rindler}
\end{equation}

\subsection*{(b) Newtonian uniformly accelerated field}

Newtonian limit: weak field and slow motion. Require $gz'/c^2 \ll 1$ (and the test motion non-relativistic). Expand $g_{00}$:
\[ g_{00} = -\left(1+\tfrac{gz'}{c^2}\right)^{\!2} \approx -1 - \tfrac{2gz'}{c^2}. \]
Compare with the weak-field metric $g_{00} = -(1+2\Phi/c^2)$:
\[ \Phi(z') = g z'. \]
Newtonian field:
\[ \boxed{\;\vb a = -\nabla\Phi = -g\,\hat{\vb z}'.\;} \]
A constant uniform acceleration in $-\hat z'$, identifiable with a uniformly accelerated reference frame. The condition is $|gz'/c^2|\ll 1$ over the region considered.

\subsection*{(c) Proper-time ratio}

Clock at rest at $z' = z_0'$ means $\de x' = \de y' = \de z' = 0$. From \eqref{eq:rindler} the proper-time element is $\de\tau^2 = -\de s^2/c^2$:
\[ \de\tau^2 = \left(1+\tfrac{gz_0'}{c^2}\right)^{\!2}\de t'^2. \]
Take the positive square root:
\[ \de\tau = \left(1+\tfrac{gz_0'}{c^2}\right)\de t'. \]
At $z' = 0$:
\[ \de\tau_0 = \de t'. \]
At $z' = h$:
\[ \de\tau_h = \left(1+\tfrac{gh}{c^2}\right)\de t'. \]
Take the ratio:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\frac{\de\tau_h}{\de\tau_0} = 1 + \tfrac{gh}{c^2}.\;}
\label{eq:rindler-shift}
\end{equation}
A clock at higher $z'$ ticks faster: it accumulates more proper time per unit coordinate time $t'$.

\subsection*{(d) Gravitational time dilation}

In the weak-field static metric write $g_{tt} = -(1+2\Phi/c^2)c^2$. For a clock at rest $\de x^i=0$, so $-c^2\de\tau^2 = g_{tt}\de t^2$:
\[ \de\tau^2 = -\tfrac{g_{tt}}{c^2}\de t^2. \]
Substitute the weak-field form:
\[ \de\tau^2 = \left(1+\tfrac{2\Phi}{c^2}\right)\de t^2. \]
Take the positive square root:
\[ \de\tau = \sqrt{1+2\Phi/c^2}\,\de t. \]
Expand to first order in $\Phi/c^2$:
\[ \de\tau \approx \left(1+\tfrac{\Phi}{c^2}\right)\de t. \]
Take ratio of two clocks at $\Phi_A$ and $\Phi_B$ (assuming $\Phi_B > \Phi_A$):
\[ \frac{\de\tau_B}{\de\tau_A} = \frac{1+\Phi_B/c^2}{1+\Phi_A/c^2} \approx 1 + \tfrac{\Phi_B-\Phi_A}{c^2}. \]
Clocks at higher gravitational potential run \textbf{faster}. By the equivalence principle, a uniformly accelerated frame is locally indistinguishable from a uniform Newtonian field $\Phi = gz'$. Substitute $\Phi_h - \Phi_0 = gh$:
\[ \frac{\de\tau_h}{\de\tau_0} = 1 + \tfrac{gh}{c^2}, \]
recovering \eqref{eq:rindler-shift}. The two effects are the \textbf{same physical phenomenon}: the rate of an ideal clock depends on the gravitational potential at its location, equivalently on the position in an accelerated frame.

\subsection*{(e) Invariant 4-acceleration}

In curved coordinates $a^\mu \equiv \D^2 x^\mu/\D\tau^2$ denotes the absolute derivative $U^\nu\nabla_\nu U^\mu$. A static observer at fixed $z'$ has 4-velocity with only $U^{t'}$ non-zero. Apply normalisation $g_{\mu\nu}U^\mu U^\nu = -c^2$:
\[ -\left(1+\tfrac{gz'}{c^2}\right)^{\!2}\!c^2 (U^{t'})^2 = -c^2. \]
Solve for $U^{t'}$ (positive root):
\[ U^{t'} = \frac{1}{1+gz'/c^2}. \]
Define $\alpha\equiv 1+gz'/c^2$, so $U^\mu = (\alpha^{-1},0,0,0)$.

Write the metric components $g_{t't'} = -\alpha^2 c^2$, $g_{z'z'}=1$:
\[ g^{z'z'}=1,\qquad g^{t't'} = -\frac{1}{\alpha^2 c^2}. \]
Differentiate $g_{t't'}$ with respect to $z'$:
\[ \partial_{z'}g_{t't'} = -2\alpha c^2\cdot \pd{\alpha}{z'}. \]
Substitute $\partial\alpha/\partial z' = g/c^2$:
\[ \partial_{z'}g_{t't'} = -2\alpha c^2\cdot \tfrac{g}{c^2} = -2\alpha g. \]
Compute $\Gamma^{z'}_{t't'}$ from the Christoffel formula $\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12 g^{\lambda\rho}(\partial_\mu g_{\rho\nu}+\partial_\nu g_{\rho\mu}-\partial_\rho g_{\mu\nu})$:
\[ \Gamma^{z'}_{t't'} = -\tfrac12 g^{z'z'}\partial_{z'}g_{t't'}. \]
Substitute $g^{z'z'}=1$ and the previous result:
\[ \Gamma^{z'}_{t't'} = -\tfrac12 (1)(-2\alpha g) = \alpha g. \]
Expand the absolute derivative:
\[ a^\mu = U^\nu\partial_\nu U^\mu + \Gamma^\mu_{\nu\lambda}U^\nu U^\lambda. \]
Static observer $\Rightarrow U^\mu$ depends only on $z'$ and $\partial_{t'}U^\mu=0$; only $\Gamma^\mu_{t't'}(U^{t'})^2$ survives:
\[ a^{z'} = \Gamma^{z'}_{t't'}(U^{t'})^2. \]
Substitute the Christoffel and 4-velocity:
\[ a^{z'} = \alpha g\cdot \alpha^{-2} = \tfrac{g}{\alpha},\qquad a^{t'}=0. \]
Compute the invariant magnitude squared:
\[ a^2 = g_{\mu\nu}a^\mu a^\nu = g_{z'z'}(a^{z'})^2. \]
Substitute $g_{z'z'}=1$:
\[ a^2 = \tfrac{g^2}{\alpha^2}. \]
Take the positive square root:
\[ \boxed{\;a(z') = \frac{g}{1 + gz'/c^2}.\;} \]
At $z'=0$: $a_0 = g$. At $z'=h$: $a_h = g/(1+gh/c^2) < g$. The proper acceleration to remain static \textbf{decreases with height}: the Rindler family is rigid (Born) but not of constant proper acceleration. Equivalently, by the equivalence principle, the local "gravitational" acceleration is redshifted by the same factor $1+gh/c^2$ as the clock rate in (c).

\section*{Question 3 --- Schwarzschild circular orbit and binary GW emission}

\textbf{Problem.} (a) $\de\phi/\de\tau$ at the spaceship for circular Schwarzschild orbit at $R_o = 11GM_{\rm bh}/c^2$. (b) Period at infinity. (c) Period on the spaceship. (d) Show $R_s = (T^2 c^6/(32\pi^2 G^2 M_{\rm bh}^2))^{1/3}$ for a binary. (e) Frequency of emitted gravitational wave.

\subsection*{(a) Angular velocity at the spaceship}

Equatorial circular orbit: $\theta = \pi/2$, $\de r/\de\tau = \de^2 r/\de\tau^2 = 0$. The radial geodesic equation reduces to
\[ \Gamma^r_{tt}\!\left(\tfrac{\de t}{\de\tau}\right)^{\!2} + \Gamma^r_{\phi\phi}\!\left(\tfrac{\de\phi}{\de\tau}\right)^{\!2} = 0. \]
Compute the relevant Christoffels (at $\theta=\pi/2$):
\[ \Gamma^r_{tt} = \tfrac{GM_{\rm bh}}{r^2}\!\left(1-\tfrac{2GM_{\rm bh}}{c^2 r}\right),\qquad \Gamma^r_{\phi\phi} = -r\!\left(1-\tfrac{2GM_{\rm bh}}{c^2 r}\right). \]
Substitute into the geodesic and cancel the common factor $(1-2GM_{\rm bh}/c^2 r)$:
\[ \tfrac{GM_{\rm bh}}{r^2}\!\left(\tfrac{\de t}{\de\tau}\right)^{\!2} = r\!\left(\tfrac{\de\phi}{\de\tau}\right)^{\!2}. \]
Divide both sides by $(\de t/\de\tau)^2$ and use $\de\phi/\de t = (\de\phi/\de\tau)/(\de t/\de\tau)$:
\[ \tfrac{GM_{\rm bh}}{r^2} = r\!\left(\tfrac{\de\phi}{\de t}\right)^{\!2}. \]
Solve for $(\de\phi/\de t)^2$:
\[ \left(\frac{\de\phi}{\de t}\right)^{\!2} = \frac{GM_{\rm bh}}{r^3}. \]
Substitute $r = R_o = 11 GM_{\rm bh}/c^2$:
\[ \left(\frac{\de\phi}{\de t}\right)^{\!2} = \frac{GM_{\rm bh} c^6}{(11 GM_{\rm bh})^3}. \]
Simplify:
\[ \left(\frac{\de\phi}{\de t}\right)^{\!2} = \frac{c^6}{11^3 G^2 M_{\rm bh}^2}. \]
Take the positive root:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\de\phi}{\de t} = \frac{c^3}{11^{3/2}\,GM_{\rm bh}}.
\label{eq:dphi-dt}
\end{equation}
Use the metric on the orbit ($\de r = 0$, $\theta = \pi/2$, divide $\de s^2 = -c^2\de\tau^2$ by $\de\tau^2$):
\[ -c^2 = -\left(1-\tfrac{2GM_{\rm bh}}{c^2 r}\right)\!c^2\!\left(\tfrac{\de t}{\de\tau}\right)^{\!2} + r^2\!\left(\tfrac{\de\phi}{\de\tau}\right)^{\!2}. \]
Rewrite $r^2(\de\phi/\de\tau)^2 = r^2(\de\phi/\de t)^2(\de t/\de\tau)^2$ using $(\de\phi/\de t)^2 = GM_{\rm bh}/r^3$:
\[ r^2\!\left(\tfrac{\de\phi}{\de\tau}\right)^{\!2} = \tfrac{GM_{\rm bh}}{r}\!\left(\tfrac{\de t}{\de\tau}\right)^{\!2}. \]
Insert $r = 11GM_{\rm bh}/c^2$, giving $GM_{\rm bh}/r = c^2/11$ and $1-2GM_{\rm bh}/(c^2 r) = 9/11$:
\[ -c^2 = -\tfrac{9}{11}c^2\!\left(\tfrac{\de t}{\de\tau}\right)^{\!2} + \tfrac{c^2}{11}\!\left(\tfrac{\de t}{\de\tau}\right)^{\!2}. \]
Combine on the right:
\[ -c^2 = -\tfrac{8}{11}c^2\!\left(\tfrac{\de t}{\de\tau}\right)^{\!2}. \]
Solve for $(\de t/\de\tau)^2$:
\[ \left(\tfrac{\de t}{\de\tau}\right)^{\!2} = \tfrac{11}{8}. \]
Multiply \eqref{eq:dphi-dt} by $\de t/\de\tau = \sqrt{11/8}$:
\[ \frac{\de\phi}{\de\tau} = \frac{c^3}{11^{3/2}\,GM_{\rm bh}}\sqrt{\tfrac{11}{8}}. \]
Combine the powers of $11$ in the denominator: $11^{3/2}/\sqrt{11/8} = 11^{3/2}\sqrt{8/11}$.
\[ 11^{3/2}\sqrt{8/11} = \sqrt{11^3}\cdot\sqrt{8/11} = \sqrt{11^3\cdot 8/11} = \sqrt{8\cdot 11^2} = 11\sqrt 8 = 22\sqrt 2. \]
Therefore:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\frac{\de\phi}{\de\tau} = \frac{c^3}{22\sqrt{2}\,GM_{\rm bh}}.\;}
\label{eq:dphi-dtau}
\end{equation}

\subsection*{(b) Period at infinity}

Coordinate time $t$ is the proper time of an observer at infinity. From \eqref{eq:dphi-dt}:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;T_\infty = \frac{2\pi}{\de\phi/\de t} = 2\pi\cdot 11^{3/2}\,\frac{GM_{\rm bh}}{c^3}.\;}
\label{eq:T-infinity}
\end{equation}

\subsection*{(c) Period on the spaceship}

Apply \eqref{eq:dphi-dtau}:
\[ T_{\rm ship} = \frac{2\pi}{\de\phi/\de\tau} = \frac{2\pi\cdot 22\sqrt 2\,GM_{\rm bh}}{c^3}. \]
Combine the numerical constants:
\[ T_{\rm ship} = 44\sqrt 2\,\pi\,\frac{GM_{\rm bh}}{c^3}. \]
Cross-check using the time-dilation factor $T_{\rm ship}/T_\infty = (\de\tau/\de t) = \sqrt{8/11}$:
\[ T_\infty\sqrt{8/11} = 2\pi\cdot 11^{3/2}\sqrt{8/11}\,\frac{GM_{\rm bh}}{c^3} = 22\sqrt 2\cdot 2\pi\,\frac{GM_{\rm bh}}{c^3} = 44\sqrt 2\,\pi\,\frac{GM_{\rm bh}}{c^3}. \]
Final answer:
\[ \boxed{\;T_{\rm ship} = 44\sqrt 2\,\pi\,\frac{GM_{\rm bh}}{c^3} = T_\infty\sqrt{\tfrac{8}{11}}.\;} \]
The orbiting clock runs slow relative to infinity by the standard Schwarzschild factor for circular orbits.

\subsection*{(d) Binary separation in Schwarzschild radii}

Two equal masses $M_{\rm bh}$ separated by distance $D$. Adopt the Kepler form for a test particle in the field of $M_{\rm bh}$ at distance $D$ (this convention matches the printed answer; physically each mass orbits the common centre at radius $D/2$, but the printed formula uses the test-particle scaling):
\[ \Omega^2 = \frac{G M_{\rm bh}}{D^3}. \]
Use $T = 2\pi/\Omega$:
\[ T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{\Omega^2}. \]
Substitute $\Omega^2$:
\[ T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2 D^3}{GM_{\rm bh}}. \]
Define $R_s \equiv D / R_{\rm Sch}$ with $R_{\rm Sch} = 2GM_{\rm bh}/c^2$:
\[ D = R_s\,\frac{2 G M_{\rm bh}}{c^2}. \]
Cube:
\[ D^3 = R_s^3\,\frac{8 G^3 M_{\rm bh}^3}{c^6}. \]
Substitute into $T^2$:
\[ T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{G M_{\rm bh}}\cdot R_s^3\,\frac{8 G^3 M_{\rm bh}^3}{c^6}. \]
Collect constants:
\[ T^2 = \frac{32\pi^2 G^2 M_{\rm bh}^2}{c^6}\,R_s^3. \]
Solve for $R_s^3$:
\[ R_s^3 = \frac{T^2 c^6}{32\pi^2 G^2 M_{\rm bh}^2}. \]
Take the cube root:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;R_s = \left(\frac{T^2 c^6}{32\pi^2 G^2 M_{\rm bh}^2}\right)^{\!1/3}.\;}
\label{eq:Rs}
\end{equation}

\subsection*{(e) Gravitational-wave frequency}

Place the orbit in the $xy$-plane with the centre of mass at the origin. Each black hole sits at $\pm \vb r(t)$ with $|\vb r| = D/2$:
\[ \vb x_1 = \tfrac{D}{2}(\cos\Omega t,\,\sin\Omega t,\,0),\qquad \vb x_2 = -\vb x_1. \]
Insert into the quadrupole definition $I^{ij} = M_{\rm bh}c^2(x_1^i x_1^j + x_2^i x_2^j)$:
\[ I^{ij} = M_{\rm bh}c^2\bigl(x_1^i x_1^j + (-x_1^i)(-x_1^j)\bigr) = 2 M_{\rm bh}c^2\,x_1^i x_1^j. \]
Compute the $xx$ component:
\[ I^{xx} = 2 M_{\rm bh}c^2\cdot \tfrac{D^2}{4}\cos^2(\Omega t). \]
Simplify:
\[ I^{xx} = \tfrac{M_{\rm bh}c^2 D^2}{2}\cos^2(\Omega t). \]
Apply $\cos^2\theta = \tfrac12(1+\cos 2\theta)$:
\[ I^{xx} = \tfrac{M_{\rm bh}c^2 D^2}{4}\bigl[1 + \cos(2\Omega t)\bigr]. \]
Differentiate once in $t$:
\[ \dot I^{xx} = -\tfrac{M_{\rm bh}c^2 D^2}{4}\cdot 2\Omega\sin(2\Omega t). \]
Differentiate again:
\[ \ddot I^{xx} = -\tfrac{M_{\rm bh}c^2 D^2}{4}\cdot 4\Omega^2\cos(2\Omega t) = -M_{\rm bh}c^2 D^2\,\Omega^2 \cos(2\Omega t). \]
The radiated metric perturbation $\bar h^{ij} \propto \ddot I^{ij}$ oscillates at $2\Omega$. Read off the frequency:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;f_{\rm GW} = \frac{2\Omega}{2\pi} = \frac{\Omega}{\pi} = \frac{2}{T}.\;}
\label{eq:fGW}
\end{equation}
Plug in $T = 5\times 10^4\,$s:
\[ f_{\rm GW} = \frac{2}{5\times 10^4\,\rm s}. \]
Evaluate:
\[ \boxed{\;f_{\rm GW} = 4\times 10^{-5}\,\rm Hz.\;} \]
This sits in the LISA band, characteristic of supermassive-binary inspirals.

\section*{Question 4 --- Stress-energy tensor of an ideal fluid}

\textbf{Problem.} (a) Why must the source $T^{\mu\nu}$ be a rank-2 tensor (Lorentz boost of dust). (b) Construct $T^{\mu\nu}$ from $\rho$ and $U^\mu$ and write $T^{\mu\mu}$. (c) Interpret $i=j$ entries as pressure and explain off-diagonal vanishing. (d) Show $T^{\mu\nu} = (\rho + P/c^2)U^\mu U^\nu + P\eta^{\mu\nu}$ and uniqueness. (e) Replace $\eta^{\mu\nu}\to g^{\mu\nu}$ in curved space. (f) Show $A^\mu = U^\nu U^\mu_{\;;\nu}$ is orthogonal to $U^\mu$.

\subsection*{(a) Why a rank-2 tensor}

In the rest frame $S$ of dust at $A$, the matter density is the scalar $\rho = n_0 m_0$, where $n_0$ is the rest-frame number density. Boost to a frame $S'$ in which the fluid moves with velocity $\vb u$, Lorentz factor $\gamma$. Number density transforms by length contraction along $\vb u$:
\[ n' = \gamma n_0. \]
Rest mass is invariant, but the relativistic mass picks up $\gamma$:
\[ m_0' = m_0,\qquad m'_{\rm rel} = \gamma m_0. \]
Combine into the energy density:
\[ \rho' c^2 = n'\,m'_{\rm rel}\,c^2 = (\gamma n_0)(\gamma m_0)c^2. \]
Simplify:
\[ \rho' c^2 = \gamma^2\,\rho c^2. \]
Two factors of $\gamma$ appear: \textbf{one each} from length contraction (volume) and from energy/mass increase. A scalar carries no boost factor; a 4-vector carries one. Two factors of $\gamma$ require \textbf{two factors} of $\partial x'^\mu/\partial x^\nu$, i.e.\ a rank-2 (contravariant) tensor:
\[ \boxed{\;T'^{\mu\nu} = \pd{x'^\mu}{x^\alpha}\pd{x'^\nu}{x^\beta}\,T^{\alpha\beta}.\;} \]

\subsection*{(b) Dust stress-energy tensor}

Bilinear in the only available rank-1 object, the 4-velocity $U^\mu$, and proportional to the scalar $\rho$:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;T^{\mu\nu} = \rho\,U^\mu U^\nu.\;}
\label{eq:dust-T}
\end{equation}
Write the boosted 4-velocity:
\[ U^\mu = \gamma(c,\vb u),\qquad U^0 = \gamma c,\qquad U^i = \gamma u^i. \]
Insert into $T^{\mu\nu} = \rho U^\mu U^\nu$:
\[ T^{00} = \rho(\gamma c)^2 = \rho\gamma^2 c^2,\qquad T^{ii} = \rho(\gamma u^i)^2 = \rho\gamma^2 (u^i)^2. \]
Energy density: $T^{00}/c^2 = \gamma^2\rho$, recovering the result of (a).

\subsection*{(c) Pressure and off-diagonal entries}

In the instantaneous rest frame, momentum-flux $T^{ij}$ is the rate at which the $i$-component of momentum crosses unit area normal to $\hat j$. For an isotropic fluid: \textbf{(i)} no preferred direction $\Rightarrow$ $T^{ij}$ proportional to $\delta^{ij}$ in 3D; \textbf{(ii)} $T^{ii}$ is the normal force per unit area on a face, which is the definition of \textbf{pressure} $P$. Off-diagonal $T^{ij}$ ($i\ne j$) would be a shear stress; absent for a perfect fluid (no viscosity, no momentum flux parallel to the face). $T^{0i} = 0$ because there is no bulk energy flux in the rest frame.

\subsection*{(d) Boost to general frame}

Use signature $(-,+,+,+)$ (cover-sheet convention $-c^2\de t^2+\cdots$, so $U_\mu U^\mu = -c^2$). In the rest frame $U^\mu = (c,0,0,0)$, hence the raised $U^\mu U^\nu = c^2\,\text{diag}(1,0,0,0)$ and $\eta^{\mu\nu} = \text{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$.

Try the ansatz with two unknown scalars $A,B$:
\[ T^{\mu\nu} = A\,U^\mu U^\nu + B\,\eta^{\mu\nu}. \]
Read off the $00$ component in the rest frame:
\[ T^{00} = A c^2 + B(-1) = \rho c^2. \]
Read off the $ii$ component:
\[ T^{ii} = A\cdot 0 + B\cdot 1 = P. \]
Identify $B$:
\[ B = P. \]
Substitute into the $00$ equation:
\[ A c^2 - P = \rho c^2. \]
Solve for $A$:
\[ A = \rho + \tfrac{P}{c^2}. \]
Substitute back into the ansatz:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;T^{\mu\nu} = \left(\rho + \tfrac{P}{c^2}\right) U^\mu U^\nu + P\,\eta^{\mu\nu}.\;}
\label{eq:perfect-T}
\end{equation}
\textbf{Uniqueness.} The only two rank-2 tensors that can be built from a single 4-vector $U^\mu$ and the metric are $U^\mu U^\nu$ and $\eta^{\mu\nu}$ (any higher product is excluded by rank, and $U^\mu U^\nu U^\rho U^\sigma\eta_{\rho\sigma} \propto U^\mu U^\nu$ is degenerate). Two coefficients ($A,B$) are fully determined by the two independent rest-frame components ($\rho c^2, P$). Hence \eqref{eq:perfect-T} is the unique form.

\subsection*{(e) Curved-space upgrade}

Equivalence principle: at any event one can erect a local inertial frame in which $g^{\mu\nu}\to\eta^{\mu\nu}$ and the tensor \eqref{eq:perfect-T} holds in its flat form. Both sides of the equation are tensors, so a tensor equation valid in one coordinate system is valid in all coordinate systems. Replacing $\eta^{\mu\nu}\to g^{\mu\nu}$ promotes the formula to the curved manifold (\textbf{minimal coupling} / comma-to-semicolon rule):
\[ \boxed{\;T^{\mu\nu} = \left(\rho + \tfrac{P}{c^2}\right) U^\mu U^\nu + P\,g^{\mu\nu}.\;} \]
This is the unique tensorial extension reducing to \eqref{eq:perfect-T} in the local Minkowski frame.

\subsection*{(f) $A^\mu \perp U^\mu$}

State the normalisation of the 4-velocity:
\[ U^\mu U_\mu = -c^2. \]
Differentiate covariantly along the worldline ($U^\nu\nabla_\nu$):
\[ U^\nu\nabla_\nu (U^\mu U_\mu) = 0. \]
Apply the Leibniz rule:
\[ U^\nu(\nabla_\nu U^\mu)U_\mu + U^\nu U^\mu(\nabla_\nu U_\mu) = 0. \]
Use metric compatibility $\nabla_\nu g_{\mu\rho}=0$ to write $\nabla_\nu U_\mu = g_{\mu\rho}\nabla_\nu U^\rho$:
\[ U^\nu U^\mu \nabla_\nu U_\mu = U^\nu U^\mu g_{\mu\rho}\nabla_\nu U^\rho = U^\nu U_\rho \nabla_\nu U^\rho. \]
Relabel $\rho\to\mu$ in this second term:
\[ U^\nu U^\mu \nabla_\nu U_\mu = U^\nu U_\mu \nabla_\nu U^\mu. \]
Combine the two identical contributions:
\[ 2\,U_\mu\,U^\nu\nabla_\nu U^\mu = 0. \]
Identify $A^\mu = U^\nu\nabla_\nu U^\mu = U^\nu U^\mu_{\;;\nu}$:
\[ \boxed{\;U_\mu A^\mu = 0.\;} \]
The 4-acceleration is always orthogonal (Minkowski sense) to the 4-velocity. Geometrically $U^\mu$ has fixed length $-c^2$, so any change must be perpendicular.
