\section*{Question 1 --- Bernoulli, hydrostatics and the sluice gate}

\subsection*{(a)}

Steady Euler, uniform $\rho$, gravity $-g\hat{\bm z}$:
\[ (\bm v\cdot\nabla)\bm v = -\frac{1}{\rho}\nabla p - g\hat{\bm z}. \]
Apply the convective identity:
\[ (\bm v\cdot\nabla)\bm v = \nabla\!\left(\tfrac12 \bm v\cdot\bm v\right) - \bm v\times(\nabla\times\bm v). \]
Write $g\hat{\bm z} = \nabla(gz)$:
\[ \nabla\!\left(\frac{\bm v\cdot\bm v}{2} + \frac{p}{\rho} + gz\right) = \bm v\times(\nabla\times\bm v). \]
Dot with $\bm v$ (RHS $\perp\bm v$):
\[ \bm v\cdot\nabla\!\left(\frac{\bm v\cdot\bm v}{2} + \frac{p}{\rho} + gz\right) = 0. \]
Directional derivative along a streamline vanishes:
\[ \boxed{\;\frac{\bm v\cdot\bm v}{2} + \frac{p}{\rho} + gz = \text{const.\ on a streamline.}\;} \]

\paragraph{Interpretation.} Energy per unit mass (kinetic + flow-work $p/\rho$ + gravitational PE) is conserved along a streamline.

\subsection*{(b)}

Gate closed, $\bm v=0$: Euler reduces to hydrostatic balance:
\[ \frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\de p}{\de z} = -g. \]
Integrate with $p(H)=p_{\text{atm}}$:
\[ p(z) = p_{\text{atm}} + \rho g(H-z). \]
Net horizontal force per width is $\int_0^H (p-p_{\text{atm}})\de z$:
\[ F_{\text{gate}} = L\!\int_0^H \rho g(H-z)\,\de z. \]
Substitute $u = H-z$:
\[ F_{\text{gate}} = L\rho g\!\int_0^H u\,\de u. \]
Evaluate:
\[ \boxed{\;F_{\text{closed}} = \frac{\rho g H^{2}L}{2}.\;} \]

\subsection*{(c)}

Bernoulli on a surface streamline, upstream $(z=H,U,p_{\text{atm}})$ to downstream $(z=h,u,p_{\text{atm}})$:
\[ \frac{U^{2}}{2} + gH = \frac{u^{2}}{2} + gh. \]
Mass conservation (equal width $L$, uniform profiles):
\[ UH = uh. \]
Eliminate $u = UH/h$:
\[ \frac{U^{2}}{2} + gH = \frac{U^{2}H^{2}}{2h^{2}} + gh. \]
Divide by $gH$, set $\hat h = h/H$, $F^{2}=U^{2}/(gH)$:
\[ \frac{F^{2}}{2} + 1 = \frac{F^{2}}{2\hat h^{2}} + \hat h. \]
Multiply by $2\hat h^{2}$:
\[ F^{2}\hat h^{2} + 2\hat h^{2} = F^{2} + 2\hat h^{3}. \]
Rearrange as cubic in $\hat h$:
\[ 2\hat h^{3} - (F^{2}+2)\hat h^{2} + F^{2} = 0. \]
Test trivial root $\hat h=1$: $2-(F^2+2)+F^2 = 0$. Factor $(\hat h-1)$:
\[ 2\hat h^{3} - (F^{2}+2)\hat h^{2} + F^{2} = (\hat h - 1)\!\left(2\hat h^{2} - F^{2}\hat h - F^{2}\right). \]
Non-trivial roots solve:
\[ 2\hat h^{2} - F^{2}\hat h - F^{2} = 0. \]
Apply the quadratic formula:
\[ \hat h = \frac{F^{2}\pm\sqrt{F^{4} + 8F^{2}}}{4}. \]
Factor $F^{2}$ from the surd, $F>0$:
\[ \hat h = \frac{F^{2}\pm F\sqrt{F^{2}+8}}{4}. \]
Reject the negative root (gives $\hat h<0$):
\[ \boxed{\;\hat h = 1 \quad\text{or}\quad \hat h = \frac{F^{2} + F\sqrt{F^{2}+8}}{4}.\;} \]

\subsection*{(d)}

Steady horizontal momentum budget over CV between upstream ($x=-X$) and downstream ($x=+X$) sections:
\[ \dot M_{\text{out}} - \dot M_{\text{in}} = \sum F_{x}^{\text{ext}}. \]
Momentum fluxes per unit width $L$, integrated over depth:
\[ \dot M_{\text{in}} = \rho U^{2}HL,\qquad \dot M_{\text{out}} = \rho u^{2}hL. \]
Use $uh=UH$, hence $u^{2}h = U^{2}H^{2}/h$:
\[ \dot M_{\text{out}} - \dot M_{\text{in}} = \rho U^{2}HL\!\left(\frac{H}{h} - 1\right). \]
Express in terms of $\hat h$:
\[ \dot M_{\text{out}} - \dot M_{\text{in}} = \frac{\rho U^{2}HL\,(1-\hat h)}{\hat h}. \]
End-section pressures are hydrostatic (parallel streamlines); collect external forces:
\[ \sum F_{x}^{\text{ext}} = \frac{\rho g L}{2}\!\left(H^{2}-h^{2}\right) - F_{\text{gate}}. \]
Equate momentum budget:
\[ \frac{\rho U^{2}HL\,(1-\hat h)}{\hat h} = \frac{\rho g L}{2}\!\left(H^{2}-h^{2}\right) - F_{\text{gate}}. \]
Solve for $F_{\text{gate}}$:
\[ F_{\text{gate}} = \frac{\rho g L}{2}\!\left(H^{2}-h^{2}\right) - \frac{\rho U^{2}HL\,(1-\hat h)}{\hat h}. \]
Factor $H^{2}-h^{2} = H^{2}(1-\hat h)(1+\hat h)$:
\[ F_{\text{gate}} = \frac{\rho g H^{2}L}{2}(1-\hat h)(1+\hat h) - \frac{\rho U^{2}HL\,(1-\hat h)}{\hat h}. \]
From (c) the quadratic gives $F^{2}(1+\hat h) = 2\hat h^{2}$:
\[ F^{2} = \frac{2\hat h^{2}}{1+\hat h}. \]
Multiply by $gH^{2}$:
\[ U^{2}H = \frac{2gH^{2}\hat h^{2}}{1+\hat h}. \]
Substitute into the momentum term:
\[ \frac{\rho U^{2}HL\,(1-\hat h)}{\hat h} = \frac{2\rho g H^{2}L\,\hat h(1-\hat h)}{1+\hat h}. \]
Factor out $\rho g H^{2}L(1-\hat h)/2$:
\[ F_{\text{gate}} = \frac{\rho g H^{2}L\,(1-\hat h)}{2}\!\left[(1+\hat h) - \frac{4\hat h}{1+\hat h}\right]. \]
Combine over $(1+\hat h)$:
\[ F_{\text{gate}} = \frac{\rho g H^{2}L\,(1-\hat h)}{2}\cdot\frac{(1+\hat h)^{2} - 4\hat h}{1+\hat h}. \]
Expand the numerator:
\[ (1+\hat h)^{2} - 4\hat h = 1 + 2\hat h + \hat h^{2} - 4\hat h. \]
Recognise $(1-\hat h)^{2}$:
\[ (1+\hat h)^{2} - 4\hat h = (1-\hat h)^{2}. \]
Substitute:
\[ \boxed{\;F_{\text{gate}} = \frac{\rho g H^{2}L}{2}\,\frac{(1-\hat h)^{3}}{1+\hat h}.\;} \]

\paragraph{Checks.} $\hat h\to 0$: recovers $\rho g H^{2}L/2$ as in (b). $\hat h\to 1$: force $\to 0$.

\section*{Question 2 --- Potential flow and the complex potential $A\sqrt z$}

\subsection*{(a)}

\textbf{Potential flow:} velocity is the gradient of a scalar potential:
\[ \bm v = \nabla\phi. \]
Vorticity vanishes identically:
\[ \bm\omega = \nabla\times\bm v = \nabla\times\nabla\phi = 0. \]
Conditions to maintain irrotationality (Kelvin's theorem):
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep0pt
\item[(i)] inviscid;
\item[(ii)] barotropic, $\nabla p\parallel\nabla\rho$;
\item[(iii)] conservative body forces;
\item[(iv)] initially irrotational.
\end{enumerate}
Incompressibility $\nabla\cdot\bm v=0$ then yields Laplace's equation:
\[ \boxed{\;\nabla^{2}\phi = 0.\;} \]

\subsection*{(b)}

Analyticity: $\de w/\de z$ direction-independent. Approach along the real axis, $\Delta z=\Delta x$:
\[ \dv{w}{z} = \pd{\phi}{x} + i\pd{\psi}{x}. \]
Approach along the imaginary axis, $\Delta z=i\Delta y$:
\[ \dv{w}{z} = \frac{1}{i}\!\left(\pd{\phi}{y} + i\pd{\psi}{y}\right). \]
Use $1/i = -i$:
\[ \dv{w}{z} = \pd{\psi}{y} - i\pd{\phi}{y}. \]
Equate real and imaginary parts:
\[ \boxed{\;\pd{\phi}{x} = \pd{\psi}{y},\qquad \pd{\psi}{x} = -\pd{\phi}{y}.\;} \]
Cross-differentiating shows both are harmonic:
\[ \nabla^{2}\phi = \nabla^{2}\psi = 0. \]

\paragraph{Use for 2D potential flow.} Incompressibility $\partial_{x}v_{x}+\partial_{y}v_{y}=0$ admits a streamfunction:
\[ v_{x} = \pd{\psi}{y},\qquad v_{y} = -\pd{\psi}{x}. \]
Irrotationality $\partial_{x}v_{y}-\partial_{y}v_{x}=0$ admits a potential:
\[ v_{x} = \pd{\phi}{x},\qquad v_{y} = \pd{\phi}{y}. \]
Comparing gives the C--R relations: every 2D potential flow has analytic $w(z)=\phi+i\psi$. Complex velocity:
\[ \dv{w}{z} = v_{x} - iv_{y}. \]
Sums, products and conformal maps of analytic functions remain analytic, building complex flow geometries from simple blocks.

\subsection*{(c)}

Write $z=re^{i\theta}$ on the principal branch, $\theta\in[0,2\pi)$:
\[ w = A\sqrt{r}\,e^{i\theta/2}. \]
Split real/imaginary parts:
\[ w = A\sqrt{r}\cos(\theta/2) + iA\sqrt{r}\sin(\theta/2). \]
Read off:
\[ \phi = A\sqrt{r}\cos(\theta/2),\qquad \boxed{\;\psi(r,\theta) = A\sqrt{r}\sin(\theta/2).\;} \]
Streamlines $\psi=C$:
\[ \sqrt{r}\sin(\theta/2) = C/A. \]
Square and set $a = C^{2}/A^{2}$:
\[ r\sin^{2}(\theta/2) = a. \]
Apply $\sin^{2}(\theta/2)=(1-\cos\theta)/2$:
\[ r(1-\cos\theta) = 2a. \]
Use $r\cos\theta = x$:
\[ r - x = 2a. \]
Square using $r^{2}=x^{2}+y^{2}$:
\[ (x+2a)^{2} = x^{2}+y^{2}. \]
Expand and simplify:
\[ y^{2} = 4a(x+a). \]
Streamlines are confocal parabolas, focus at origin, sharing $\psi=0$ on the positive $x$-axis.

\paragraph{Physical flow.} Flow around a semi-infinite plate (knife-edge) on the positive $x$-axis, fluid wraps around the tip at the origin. Complex velocity:
\[ \dv{w}{z} = \frac{A}{2\sqrt{z}} = \frac{A}{2\sqrt{r}}e^{-i\theta/2}. \]
Speed diverges at the tip:
\[ |\bm v| = \frac{A}{2\sqrt{r}}. \]

\paragraph{Paddle wheel near $\theta=2\pi^{-}$.} Vorticity $\omega = 0$ in the bulk: the wheel \emph{does not spin}, only advects. At $\theta\to 2\pi^{-}$, $e^{-i\theta/2}\to -1$:
\[ v_{x} - iv_{y} = -\frac{A}{2\sqrt{r}}. \]
So $v_{x}<0$, $v_{y}=0$: wheel drifts along the underside towards the tip, whips around, and emerges along $\theta=0^{+}$ with body axes fixed in the lab frame.

\subsection*{(d)}

Reynolds number:
\[ \boxed{\;\mathrm{Re} = \frac{UL}{\nu}.\;} \]
Compare Navier--Stokes terms:
\[ \frac{\text{inertial}}{\text{viscous}} = \frac{U^{2}/L}{\nu U/L^{2}} = \mathrm{Re}. \]
$\mathrm{Re}\gg 1$: inertia dominates, viscosity confined to thin boundary layers. $\mathrm{Re}\ll 1$: viscous (Stokes) flow.

\paragraph{Two differences from high-Re real flow.}

\textit{(i) Boundary layers.} Potential flow allows slip; real fluid demands $\bm v=0$ at the wall. Viscosity sets a thin layer of thickness:
\[ \delta \sim L\,\mathrm{Re}^{-1/2} \ll L. \]
Wall-bounded vorticity $\sim U/\delta$ is continuously generated and convected downstream.

\textit{(ii) Separation and wake.} Potential flow demands $|\bm v|\propto r^{-1/2}\to\infty$ at the tip. The real boundary layer cannot withstand the adverse pressure gradient: it \emph{separates} at the tip, producing a turbulent wake of recirculating vortical fluid.

\paragraph{Real paddle wheel.} Boundary layer vorticity $\omega\sim U/\delta\neq 0$ makes the wheel \emph{spin} as it is convected. At the tip the boundary layer separates, shedding the wheel into the wake where it continues to tumble.

\paragraph{Why $\mathrm{Re}\to\infty$ fails to recover potential flow.} Viscosity $\nu\nabla^{2}\bm v$ is a \emph{singular perturbation}: it contains the highest spatial derivative. Dropping it lowers the PDE order, dropping the no-slip BC. With any $\nu>0$, the boundary layer exists: $\delta\sim L\,\mathrm{Re}^{-1/2}\to 0$, vorticity $\mathcal O(U/\delta)\to\infty$. Shed circulation per unit time stays $\mathcal O(1)$, producing wakes that persist in the limit (d'Alembert's paradox: finite drag at any $\mathrm{Re}$).

\section*{Question 3 --- Internal gravity waves}

\subsection*{(a)}

\paragraph{Assumptions.} (i) Parcel keeps its own density $\rho_{p}=\rho_{0}(z_{0})$ (incompressible, adiabatic). (ii) Pressure equilibrium (acoustic timescale $\ll$ buoyancy timescale). (iii) Small displacement $\zeta$, Taylor-expand $\rho_{0}$.

Newton's law per unit volume (buoyancy is net force):
\[ \rho_{p}\,\ddot\zeta = -\bigl[\rho_{p} - \rho_{0}(z_{0}+\zeta)\bigr]g. \]
Taylor-expand the background density:
\[ \rho_{0}(z_{0}+\zeta) \approx \rho_{0}(z_{0}) + \zeta\,\dv{\rho_{0}}{z}. \]
Use $\rho_{p}=\rho_{0}(z_{0})$:
\[ \rho_{p} - \rho_{0}(z_{0}+\zeta) = -\zeta\,\dv{\rho_{0}}{z}. \]
Substitute into Newton's law:
\[ \rho_{0}(z_{0})\,\ddot\zeta = \zeta\,g\,\dv{\rho_{0}}{z}. \]
Divide by $\rho_{0}$:
\[ \ddot\zeta - \frac{g}{\rho_{0}}\dv{\rho_{0}}{z}\zeta = 0. \]
Define $N^{2} = -(g/\rho_{0})\,\de\rho_{0}/\de z$:
\[ \ddot\zeta + N^{2}\zeta = 0. \]
Read off the buoyancy frequency:
\[ \boxed{\;N = \sqrt{-\frac{g}{\rho_{0}}\,\dv{\rho_{0}}{z}}.\;} \]

\subsection*{(b)}

Decompose into background + perturbation:
\[ \rho = \rho_{0}(z) + \delta\rho,\qquad p = p_{0}(z) + \delta p. \]
Background hydrostatic balance:
\[ \dv{p_{0}}{z} = -\rho_{0}g. \]
Linearise Euler about rest, drop $\bm v\cdot\nabla\bm v$:
\[ \rho_{0}\pd{\bm v}{t} = -\nabla\delta p - \delta\rho\,g\hat{\bm z}. \]
Take the $x$-component:
\[ \rho_{0}\pd{v_{x}}{t} = -\pd{\delta p}{x}. \]
Take the $z$-component:
\[ \rho_{0}\pd{v_{z}}{t} = -\pd{\delta p}{z} - \delta\rho\,g. \]
Take $\partial_{z}$ of the $x$-equation and $\partial_{x}$ of the $z$-equation, subtract:
\[ \pd{}{z}\!\left(\rho_{0}\pd{v_{x}}{t}\right) - \pd{}{x}\!\left(\rho_{0}\pd{v_{z}}{t}\right) = g\,\pd{\delta\rho}{x}. \]
Expand the LHS noting $\partial_{x}\rho_{0}=0$:
\[ \rho_{0}\,\pd{}{t}\!\left(\pd{v_{x}}{z} - \pd{v_{z}}{x}\right) + \dv{\rho_{0}}{z}\pd{v_{x}}{t} = g\,\pd{\delta\rho}{x}. \]
Boussinesq: drop $\de\rho_{0}/\de z$ term (small by $N^{2}/g$):
\[ \rho_{0}\,\pd{}{t}\!\left(\pd{v_{x}}{z} - \pd{v_{z}}{x}\right) = g\,\pd{\delta\rho}{x}. \]
Divide by $\rho_{0}$ and use $b = -g\,\delta\rho/\rho_{0}$:
\[ \boxed{\;\pd{}{t}\!\left(\pd{v_{x}}{z} - \pd{v_{z}}{x}\right) = -\pd{b}{x}.\;} \]

\paragraph{Interpretation.} Horizontal buoyancy gradients tilt isopycnals and generate vorticity $\omega_{y} = \partial_{z}v_{x}-\partial_{x}v_{z}$ (baroclinic overturning).

\subsection*{(c)}

Incompressibility $\partial_{x}v_{x}+\partial_{z}v_{z}=0$ admits a streamfunction:
\[ v_{x} = \pd{\psi}{z},\qquad v_{z} = -\pd{\psi}{x}. \]
Vorticity becomes:
\[ \pd{v_{x}}{z} - \pd{v_{z}}{x} = \pdd{\psi}{z} + \pdd{\psi}{x} = \nabla^{2}\psi. \]
Substitute into (b):
\[ \pd{}{t}\nabla^{2}\psi = -\pd{b}{x}. \]
Linearise $D\rho/Dt = 0$ about $\rho_{0}(z)$:
\[ \pd{\delta\rho}{t} + v_{z}\dv{\rho_{0}}{z} = 0. \]
Multiply by $-g/\rho_{0}$:
\[ \pd{b}{t} = \frac{g}{\rho_{0}}\dv{\rho_{0}}{z}\,v_{z}. \]
Use $N^{2} = -(g/\rho_{0})\,\de\rho_{0}/\de z$:
\[ \pd{b}{t} = -N^{2}v_{z}. \]
Substitute $v_{z} = -\partial_{x}\psi$:
\[ \pd{b}{t} = N^{2}\pd{\psi}{x}. \]
Differentiate the vorticity equation in $t$:
\[ \pdd{}{t}\nabla^{2}\psi = -\pd{}{x}\!\pd{b}{t}. \]
Insert $\partial_{t}b = N^{2}\partial_{x}\psi$:
\[ \boxed{\;\pdd{}{t}\!\left(\pdd{\psi}{x} + \pdd{\psi}{z}\right) = -N^{2}\pdd{\psi}{x}.\;} \]

\subsection*{(d)}

Plane-wave ansatz $\psi \propto e^{i(\bm k\cdot\bm r - \omega t)}$:
\[ \pd{}{x}\to ik_{x},\quad \pd{}{z}\to ik_{z},\quad \pd{}{t}\to -i\omega. \]
Substitute into the wave equation:
\[ (-i\omega)^{2}\bigl[(ik_{x})^{2} + (ik_{z})^{2}\bigr]\psi = -N^{2}(ik_{x})^{2}\psi. \]
Use $(-i\omega)^{2}=-\omega^{2}$, $(ik_{j})^{2}=-k_{j}^{2}$, $k^{2}=k_{x}^{2}+k_{z}^{2}$:
\[ \omega^{2}k^{2} = N^{2}k_{x}^{2}. \]
Divide by $k^{2}$, set $\cos\theta = k_{x}/k$:
\[ \boxed{\;\omega^{2} = N^{2}\,\frac{k_{x}^{2}}{k_{x}^{2}+k_{z}^{2}} = N^{2}\cos^{2}\theta.\;} \]
Frequency depends on direction only, bounded by $N$.

\paragraph{Group velocity.} Take $\omega>0$, $k_{x}>0$, so $\omega = Nk_{x}/k$. Quotient rule:
\[ \pd{\omega}{k_{x}} = \frac{N}{k} - \frac{Nk_{x}^{2}}{k^{3}}. \]
Simplify using $k^{2}-k_{x}^{2}=k_{z}^{2}$:
\[ \pd{\omega}{k_{x}} = \frac{Nk_{z}^{2}}{k^{3}}. \]
Differentiate w.r.t.\ $k_{z}$:
\[ \pd{\omega}{k_{z}} = -\frac{Nk_{x}k_{z}}{k^{3}}. \]
Assemble:
\[ \bm c_{g} = \frac{N k_{z}}{k^{3}}\bigl(k_{z}\hat{\bm x} - k_{x}\hat{\bm z}\bigr). \]
Dot with $\bm k$:
\[ \bm c_{g}\cdot\bm k = \frac{Nk_{z}}{k^{3}}\bigl(k_{x}k_{z} - k_{x}k_{z}\bigr) = 0. \]
Hence:
\[ \boxed{\;\bm c_{g}\perp\bm k,\;} \]
i.e.\ $\bm c_{g}\perp\bm c_{p}$ since $\bm c_{p}\parallel\bm k$. Magnitude:
\[ |\bm c_{g}| = \frac{N|k_{z}|}{k^{2}} = \frac{N|\sin\theta|}{k}. \]

\section*{Question 4 --- Thin-film viscous flow on an inclined plane}

\subsection*{(a)}

Axes: $x$ down-slope, $z$ normal; plate $z=0$, surface $z=h(x,t)$. Gravity:
\[ \bm g = g\sin\theta\,\hat{\bm x} - g\cos\theta\,\hat{\bm z}. \]
\paragraph{Lubrication assumptions.} (i) Thin film $\varepsilon\equiv h/L\ll 1$. (ii) Reduced Reynolds $\varepsilon\,\mathrm{Re}\ll 1$ (inertia negligible). (iii) Incompressible.

Compare viscous second derivatives:
\[ \pdd{u}{x}\sim\frac{U}{L^{2}},\qquad \pdd{u}{z}\sim\frac{U}{h^{2}}. \]
Ratio $h^{2}/L^{2}=\varepsilon^{2}$: keep $\partial_{z}^{2}u$ only. Steady NS along the plate:
\[ \boxed{\;0 = -\frac{1}{\rho}\pd{p}{x} + g\sin\theta + \nu\,\pdd{u}{z}.\;} \]
Normal to the plate (all dynamical terms negligible):
\[ \boxed{\;0 = -\frac{1}{\rho}\pd{p}{z} - g\cos\theta.\;} \]

\paragraph{Boundary conditions.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
\item No-slip: $u(x,0,t)=0,\ w(x,0,t)=0$.
\item No tangential stress at free surface: $\partial_{z}u|_{z=h}=0$.
\item Atmospheric pressure at free surface: $p(x,h,t)=p_{\text{atm}}$.
\item Kinematic condition: $\partial_{t}h + u\,\partial_{x}h = w$ at $z=h$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{(b)}

Integrate $\partial_{z}p = -\rho g\cos\theta$ from $z$ to $h$ with $p(h)=p_{\text{atm}}$:
\[ p(x,z,t) = p_{\text{atm}} + \rho g\cos\theta\,[h(x,t) - z]. \]
Differentiate in $x$:
\[ \pd{p}{x} = \rho g\cos\theta\,\pd{h}{x}. \]
Substitute into $x$-momentum:
\[ \nu\,\pdd{u}{z} = \frac{1}{\rho}\pd{p}{x} - g\sin\theta. \]
Simplify:
\[ \nu\,\pdd{u}{z} = -g\!\left(\sin\theta - \cos\theta\,\pd{h}{x}\right). \]
Integrate once in $z$:
\[ \nu\,\pd{u}{z} = -g\!\left(\sin\theta - \cos\theta\,\pd{h}{x}\right)z + C_{1}. \]
Apply $\partial_{z}u|_{z=h}=0$:
\[ C_{1} = gh\!\left(\sin\theta - \cos\theta\,\pd{h}{x}\right). \]
Substitute back:
\[ \nu\,\pd{u}{z} = g\!\left(\sin\theta - \cos\theta\,\pd{h}{x}\right)(h - z). \]
Integrate again, $C_{2}=0$ from $u(0)=0$:
\[ \boxed{\;u(x,z,t) = \frac{g}{\nu}\!\left(\sin\theta - \cos\theta\,\pd{h}{x}\right)\!\left(hz - \tfrac{1}{2}z^{2}\right).\;} \]

\paragraph{Volumetric flux per unit width.}
\[ q(x,t) = \int_{0}^{h}\!u\,\de z. \]
Pull out prefactor:
\[ q = \frac{g}{\nu}\!\left(\sin\theta - \cos\theta\,\pd{h}{x}\right)\!\int_{0}^{h}\!\left(hz - \tfrac12 z^{2}\right)\de z. \]
Evaluate the integral:
\[ \int_{0}^{h}\!\left(hz - \tfrac12 z^{2}\right)\de z = \tfrac12 h^{3} - \tfrac16 h^{3} = \tfrac{h^{3}}{3}. \]
Hence:
\[ q = \frac{gh^{3}}{3\nu}\!\left(\sin\theta - \cos\theta\,\pd{h}{x}\right). \]

\paragraph{Mass conservation.} Integrate $\partial_{x}u + \partial_{z}w = 0$ from $0$ to $h$, $w(0)=0$:
\[ w(h) = -\!\int_{0}^{h}\!\pd{u}{x}\,\de z. \]
Apply Leibniz on $q$:
\[ \pd{q}{x} = \int_{0}^{h}\!\pd{u}{x}\,\de z + u(x,h,t)\,\pd{h}{x}. \]
Combine:
\[ w(h) = -\pd{q}{x} + u(x,h,t)\,\pd{h}{x}. \]
Apply the kinematic BC $\partial_{t}h + u(h)\partial_{x}h = w(h)$:
\[ \pd{h}{t} = -\pd{q}{x}. \]
Substitute $q$:
\[ \boxed{\;\pd{h}{t} + \pd{}{x}\!\left[\frac{gh^{3}}{3\nu}\!\left(\sin\theta - \cos\theta\,\pd{h}{x}\right)\right] = 0.\;} \]

\subsection*{(c)}

For $\theta=\mathcal O(1)$ with $\cos\theta\,|\partial_{x}h|\ll\sin\theta$, drop the diffusive correction:
\[ \pd{h}{t} + \pd{}{x}\!\left(\frac{gh^{3}\sin\theta}{3\nu}\right) = 0. \]
Apply the chain rule:
\[ \pd{h}{t} + \frac{g\sin\theta}{3\nu}\,3h^{2}\pd{h}{x} = 0. \]
Recast as quasilinear:
\[ \pd{h}{t} + u(h,\theta)\pd{h}{x} = 0, \]
with the kinematic-wave speed
\[ \boxed{\;u(h,\theta) = \frac{gh^{2}\sin\theta}{\nu}.\;} \]
This equals the surface velocity $u(z=h)$ from (b), three times the depth-averaged $\bar u = q/h = gh^{2}\sin\theta/(3\nu)$.

Solve by characteristics. On $\de x/\de t = u(h,\theta)$:
\[ \dv{h}{t} = \pd{h}{t} + u\,\pd{h}{x} = 0. \]
So $h$ is constant along straight characteristics:
\[ x = x_{0} + u(h_{0},\theta)\,t. \]
Eliminate $x_{0}$:
\[ \boxed{\;h(x,t) \approx h_{0}\!\left(x - \frac{gh^{2}\sin\theta}{\nu}t\right).\;} \]

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Bump evolution.} Wave speed $u(h,\theta) \propto h^{2}$ increases with $h$. Thicker fluid catches up with thinner fluid ahead, so the forward face \emph{steepens}; the rear face \emph{flattens}:

\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=1.0]
\draw[->] (-0.5,0) -- (8.5,0) node[right] {$x$};
\draw[->] (-0.5,0) -- (-0.5,1.7) node[above] {$h$};
% initial profile (smooth bump)
\draw[thick,blue,domain=-0.2:6.0,smooth,samples=80] plot (\x,{0.3 + 1.0*exp(-(\x-2.5)^2)});
\node[blue] at (2.5,1.55) {\small $t=0$};
% later: steepened front, flattened back
\draw[thick,red,domain=-0.2:7.7,smooth,samples=160] plot (\x,{0.3 + 1.0*exp(-((\x-4.5)^2)/((\x<4.5)*1.8 + (\x>=4.5)*0.25 + 0.0001 ))});
\node[red] at (5.6,1.4) {\small $t>0$};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
Characteristics from the leading slope cross at a finite time $t^{*}$: $\partial_{x}h$ becomes singular (shock). At that moment the kinematic-wave approximation breaks down, since the neglected $\cos\theta\,\partial_{x}h$ term is no longer small and regularises the shock into a finite-width front.
