\section*{Question 1 --- Crystal structure of tungsten}

\textbf{Problem.} Identify lattice types of $\alpha$- and $\beta$-tungsten; from the lowest-angle powder XRD ring of $\alpha$-W (\SI{0.1542}{nm} X-rays, $2\theta=40.26^\circ$) deduce $a$ and the density; for $\beta$-W find the first two non-vanishing rings, their intensity ratio, and the lowest-angle additional weak ring expected.

\subsection*{(a) Definition and lattice type of $\alpha$-W}

A \emph{lattice} is the infinite set of points
\[
\bm R = n_1\bm a_1 + n_2\bm a_2 + n_3\bm a_3,\qquad n_i\in\mathbb Z,
\]
generated by three primitive vectors $\bm a_i$; every lattice point has identical environment.

For $\alpha$-W, the two atoms at $[0,0,0]$ and $[\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac12]$ are chemically identical and have identical local environments, so each is a lattice point. The conventional cubic cell with the centring vector $[\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac12]$ is
\[
\boxed{\;\text{body-centred cubic (BCC).}\;}
\]

\subsection*{(b) Lattice constant and density}

Selection rule for BCC: reflections allowed when $h+k+l$ is even.

Identify the lowest-$\sin\theta$ ring:
\[
\text{lowest-angle ring} = (110),\quad h^2+k^2+l^2 = 2.
\]
Bragg's law:
\[
\lambda = 2 d_{110}\sin\theta.
\]
Solve for $d_{110}$:
\[
d_{110} = \frac{\lambda}{2\sin\theta}.
\]
Halve the scattering angle $2\theta=40.26^\circ$:
\[
\theta = 20.13^\circ.
\]
Evaluate $\sin\theta$:
\[
\sin(20.13^\circ) = 0.3441.
\]
Substitute the numerical values:
\[
d_{110} = \frac{\SI{0.1542}{nm}}{2\times 0.3441}.
\]
Evaluate:
\[
d_{110} = \SI{0.2240}{nm}.
\]
Cubic spacing $d_{hkl}=a/\sqrt{h^2+k^2+l^2}$ gives:
\[
a = d_{110}\sqrt{2}.
\]
Substitute:
\[
a = (\SI{0.2240}{nm})\sqrt{2}.
\]
Evaluate:
\[
\boxed{\;a = \SI{0.3168}{nm}.\;}
\]

BCC conventional cell contains 2 atoms. Mass density:
\[
\rho = \frac{2 M}{N_A a^3}.
\]
Substitute the numerical values:
\[
\rho = \frac{2 \times \SI{0.1838}{kg\,mol^{-1}}}{(\SI{6.022e23}{mol^{-1}})\,(\SI{3.168e-10}{m})^3}.
\]
Evaluate $a^3$:
\[
a^3 = \SI{3.179e-29}{m^3}.
\]
Combine numerator and denominator:
\[
\rho = \frac{\SI{0.3676}{kg\,mol^{-1}}}{\SI{1.914e-5}{m^3\,mol^{-1}}}.
\]
Evaluate:
\[
\boxed{\;\rho \approx \SI{1.92e4}{kg\,m^{-3}} = \SI{19.2}{g\,cm^{-3}}.\;}
\]

\subsection*{(c) Lattice type of $\beta$-W}

The two corner/centre atoms $\{[0,0,0],[\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac12]\}$ have a different local environment from the six face-pair atoms $[\tfrac14,\tfrac12,0]$ etc., so the eight sites are \emph{not} all equivalent: they cannot all be lattice points.

Test BCC centring $\bm T=[\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac12]$:
\[
[\tfrac14,\tfrac12,0] + [\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac12] = [\tfrac34,1,\tfrac12]\equiv [\tfrac34,0,\tfrac12],
\]
which is not in the basis: BCC ruled out. FCC and primitive face centring similarly fail. Hence
\[
\boxed{\;\text{simple (primitive) cubic, with an 8-atom basis (A15 structure).}\;}
\]

\subsection*{(d) First two rings of $\beta$-W and their intensity ratio}

All eight atoms have the same form factor $f$. Structure factor:
\[
S_{hkl} = f\sum_{j=1}^{8} e^{2\pi i(h x_j + k y_j + l z_j)}.
\]
Corner pair $[0,0,0],[\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac12]$:
\[
S_c/f = 1 + e^{i\pi(h+k+l)}.
\]
Use $e^{i\pi n}=(-1)^n$:
\[
S_c/f = 1 + (-1)^{h+k+l}.
\]
Face pair on the $z=0$ face, $[\tfrac14,\tfrac12,0]+[\tfrac34,\tfrac12,0]$:
\[
S_{f,z}/f = e^{2\pi i(h/4+k/2)}+e^{2\pi i(3h/4+k/2)}.
\]
Factor out $e^{i\pi k}$:
\[
S_{f,z}/f = e^{i\pi k}\bigl(e^{i\pi h/2}+e^{i3\pi h/2}\bigr).
\]
Factor $e^{i\pi h}$ from the bracket:
\[
e^{i\pi h/2}+e^{i3\pi h/2} = e^{i\pi h}\bigl(e^{-i\pi h/2}+e^{i\pi h/2}\bigr).
\]
Apply $e^{i\theta}+e^{-i\theta}=2\cos\theta$:
\[
e^{i\pi h/2}+e^{i3\pi h/2} = 2 e^{i\pi h}\cos(\pi h/2).
\]
Substitute back:
\[
S_{f,z}/f = 2(-1)^{h+k}\cos(\pi h/2).
\]
Repeat the same factorisation for the pairs on the $y=0$ face ($[\tfrac12,0,\tfrac14],[\tfrac12,0,\tfrac34]$) and on the $x=0$ face ($[0,\tfrac14,\tfrac12],[0,\tfrac34,\tfrac12]$). Sum the three face-pair contributions:
\[
S_f/f = 2(-1)^{h+k}\cos\tfrac{\pi h}{2} + 2(-1)^{h+l}\cos\tfrac{\pi l}{2} + 2(-1)^{k+l}\cos\tfrac{\pi k}{2}.
\]
Combine corner and face contributions:
\[
S/f = \bigl[1+(-1)^{h+k+l}\bigr] + 2\bigl[(-1)^{h+k}\cos\tfrac{\pi h}{2} + (-1)^{h+l}\cos\tfrac{\pi l}{2} + (-1)^{k+l}\cos\tfrac{\pi k}{2}\bigr].
\]

Evaluate for low $(hkl)$:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{cccc}
\toprule
$(hkl)$ & $h^2+k^2+l^2$ & $S/f$ & $m$ \\
\midrule
(100) & 1 & $0+2[0-1+1]=0$ & --- \\
(110) & 2 & $2+2[0-1+0]=0$ & --- \\
(111) & 3 & $0+2[0+0+0]=0$ & --- \\
(200) & 4 & $2+2[-1+1+1]=4$ & 6 \\
(210) & 5 & $0+2[1+1+0]=4$ & 24 \\
(211) & 6 & $2+2[1+0+0]=4$ & 24 \\
(220) & 8 & $2+2[-1+1-1]=0$ & --- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

First two non-vanishing rings:
\[
\boxed{\;(200)\ \text{and}\ (210).\;}
\]

Both have $|S|^2 = 16 f^2$, so the ratio is set by multiplicity (Lorentz-polarisation and Debye-Waller corrections are subleading):
\[
\frac{I_{210}}{I_{200}} \approx \frac{m_{210}}{m_{200}} = \frac{24}{6}.
\]
\[
\boxed{\;\frac{I_{210}}{I_{200}} \approx 4.\;}
\]

\subsection*{(e) Additional weak ring at lower angle}

The cancellations producing $|S|=0$ assumed all 8 sites scatter identically. The two Wyckoff sites are crystallographically distinct (corner sublattice vs.\ face-pair sublattice): they vibrate differently, giving distinct anisotropic Debye--Waller factors and slightly different effective form factors, $f_1\neq f_2$.

Write $S = f_1 S_c + f_2 S_f$ with $S_c = 1+(-1)^{h+k+l}$ and $S_f$ the face-pair sum. Tabulating $(S_c, S_f)$ for the previously forbidden rings:
\[
(100):\ (0,0),\qquad (110):\ (2,-2),\qquad (111):\ (0,0),\qquad (220):\ (2,-2).
\]
Rings with $S_c=S_f=0$ remain extinct; rings with $S_c\ne 0$ and $S_f\ne 0$ acquire weight $\propto (f_1-f_2)^2$.

Lowest such ring (smallest $h^2+k^2+l^2$):
\[
\boxed{\;(110),\;}
\]
seen weakly because $|f_1-f_2|\ll f$.

\section*{Question 2 --- Tight binding}

\textbf{Problem.} Define $\mu$ and $E_F$. For a 1D tight-binding chain with hopping $-t$, derive the dispersion, sketch it and the DOS. For monovalent atoms find $E_F$ and show $\mu(T)=E_F$. Repeat the Fermi-surface and $\mu(T)$ analysis on a 2D square lattice, then on a squeezed rectangular lattice.

\subsection*{(a) Definitions}

Chemical potential: $\mu = \left(\partial U/\partial N\right)_{S,V}$, the energy cost of adding one particle at fixed entropy and volume; equivalently, the energy at which the Fermi--Dirac function equals $\tfrac12$.

Fermi energy: $E_F = \mu(T=0)$, the energy of the highest occupied single-particle state in the ground state of a non-interacting fermion system.

\subsection*{(b) 1D dispersion, BZ diagram, DOS}

Expand a single-particle eigenstate $\ket{\psi}=\sum_n \psi_n\ket{n}$. Apply $H$:
\[
H\ket{\psi} = \sum_n \psi_n\bigl(\epsilon_0\ket{n} - t\ket{n+1} - t\ket{n-1}\bigr).
\]
Project onto $\bra{m}$ (use $\braket{m|n}=\delta_{mn}$):
\[
E\,\psi_m = \epsilon_0\,\psi_m - t\,\psi_{m-1} - t\,\psi_{m+1}.
\]
Substitute Bloch ansatz $\psi_n = e^{ikna}$:
\[
E\,e^{ikma} = \epsilon_0\,e^{ikma} - t\,e^{ik(m-1)a} - t\,e^{ik(m+1)a}.
\]
Divide both sides by $e^{ikma}$:
\[
E = \epsilon_0 - t\,e^{-ika} - t\,e^{ika}.
\]
Combine exponentials with $e^{i\theta}+e^{-i\theta}=2\cos\theta$:
\[
\boxed{\;E(k) = \epsilon_0 - 2t\cos(ka),\qquad k\in(-\pi/a,\pi/a].\;}
\label{eq:tb1d}
\]

DOS per spin in 1D: states in length $L$ between $k$ and $k+\de k$ number $(L/2\pi)\de k$. With two $k$ branches ($\pm k$) at each $E$:
\[
g(E)\,\de E = 2\cdot\frac{L}{2\pi}|\de k| = \frac{L}{\pi}\frac{\de E}{|\de E/\de k|}.
\]
Compute $|\de E/\de k|$ from the dispersion:
\[
\Bigl|\frac{\de E}{\de k}\Bigr| = 2ta|\sin(ka)|.
\]
Use $\sin^2(ka) = 1 - \cos^2(ka)$ with $\cos(ka) = (\epsilon_0-E)/(2t)$:
\[
|\sin(ka)| = \frac{1}{2t}\sqrt{(2t)^2 - (E-\epsilon_0)^2}.
\]
Substitute and invert:
\[
g(E) = \frac{L}{\pi a}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(2t)^2 - (E-\epsilon_0)^2}}.
\]
Van Hove singularities at $E=\epsilon_0\pm 2t$.

\subsection*{(c) Half-filling: $E_F$ and $\mu(T)$}

Bandwidth $4t$, $N$ atoms, $2N$ spin states, $N$ electrons (monovalent): half-filled. Symmetry $E(k+\pi/a)-\epsilon_0 = -[E(k)-\epsilon_0]$ gives $g(\epsilon_0+\delta)=g(\epsilon_0-\delta)$.

At $T=0$, fill the lower half of the band:
\[
\boxed{\;E_F = \epsilon_0.\;}
\]

Particle-number constraint per spin at temperature $T$:
\[
N_e^{(\sigma)} = \int g(E)\,n_F(E,\mu,T)\,\de E.
\]
Half-filling sets the per-spin filling to half the band:
\[
N_e^{(\sigma)} = \tfrac12\int g(E)\,\de E.
\]
Apply the hint $n_F(E,\mu) = 1 - n_F(2\mu - E,\mu)$:
\[
\int g(E)\,n_F(E,\mu)\,\de E = \int g(E)\,\de E - \int g(E)\,n_F(2\mu - E,\mu)\,\de E.
\]
Change variables $E' = 2\mu - E$ in the second integral:
\[
\int g(E)\,n_F(2\mu-E)\,\de E = \int g(2\mu-E')\,n_F(E')\,\de E'.
\]
Substitute back (rename $E'\to E$):
\[
\tfrac12\int g(E)\,\de E = \int g(E)\,\de E - \int g(2\mu-E)\,n_F(E,\mu)\,\de E.
\]
The DOS satisfies $g(2\epsilon_0 - E) = g(E)$ by the $E\to 2\epsilon_0 - E$ symmetry of the cosine band. Demand consistency by setting $\mu = \epsilon_0$:
\[
\int g(2\epsilon_0-E)\,n_F(E,\epsilon_0)\,\de E = \int g(E)\,n_F(E,\epsilon_0)\,\de E = N_e = \tfrac12\int g\,\de E.
\]
The constraint is then identically satisfied, fixing:
\[
\boxed{\;\mu(T) = \epsilon_0 = E_F\quad\text{for all }T.\;}
\]
Particle--hole symmetry pins $\mu$ to the band centre.

\subsection*{(d) 2D square lattice, monovalent}

Brillouin zone: square $-\pi/a < k_x,k_y \le \pi/a$. Half-filling sets $\cos(k_x a) + \cos(k_y a) = 0$: a square rotated by $45^\circ$, vertices $(\pm\pi/a,0),(0,\pm\pi/a)$.

\[
\boxed{\;\mu(T) = \epsilon_0\quad\text{for all }T.\;}
\]

\subsection*{(e) Squeezed rectangular lattice, $a_x < a_y$}

Brillouin zone is rectangular: $-\pi/a_i < k_i \le \pi/a_i$, wider along $k_x$ since $\pi/a_x>\pi/a_y$. Anisotropic dispersion:
\[
E(k_x,k_y) = \epsilon_0 - 2t_x\cos(k_x a_x) - 2t_y\cos(k_y a_y).
\]
Shorter bond gives stronger overlap so $t_x > t_y$. Half-filling sits at $E = \epsilon_0$:
\[
t_x\cos(k_x a_x) + t_y\cos(k_y a_y) = 0.
\]
On the $k_y=0$ line, solve $\cos(k_x a_x) = -t_y/t_x$. Since $|t_y/t_x|<1$:
\[
k_x = \frac{1}{a_x}\arccos\!\Bigl(-\frac{t_y}{t_x}\Bigr) < \frac{\pi}{a_x}.
\]
On the $k_x=0$ line, $\cos(k_ya_y) = -t_x/t_y$. Since $|t_x/t_y|>1$ there is no real solution: the Fermi surface does not cross the $k_y$ axis. Instead it reaches the BZ edge $k_y=\pi/a_y$ at:
\[
k_x = \frac{1}{a_x}\arccos\!\Bigl(\frac{t_y}{t_x}\Bigr).
\]
Consequently the Fermi surface is \emph{open}: it runs across the BZ in the $k_x$ sense, exiting at $k_y=\pm\pi/a_y$ and reconnecting by zone-boundary periodicity.

\section*{Question 3 --- Phonons in lead}

\textbf{Problem.} Define phonon and Brillouin zone. From the Morse potential ($\xi=\SI{0.084}{nm},\,A=\SI{1600}{eV},\,B=\SI{40}{eV}$) compute the equilibrium bond length $a$, binding energy and harmonic spring constant $\kappa$. Compute the 1D sound velocity (Pb, $M=\SI{207}{g\,mol^{-1}}$). Estimate the thermal-expansion coefficient using cubic anharmonicity, and identify the validity ranges in $T$.

\subsection*{(a) Definitions}

Phonon: a quantised normal mode of lattice vibration carrying energy $\hbar\omega$ and crystal momentum $\hbar\bm k$, obeying Bose--Einstein statistics.

Brillouin zone: the Wigner--Seitz primitive cell of the reciprocal lattice; equivalently, the set of $\bm k$ closer to the origin than to any other reciprocal lattice point.

\subsection*{(b) Morse: $a$, $V_{\min}$, $\kappa$}

Set $u\equiv e^{-r/\xi}$:
\[
V = A u^2 - B u.
\]
Apply chain rule with $\de u/\de r = -u/\xi$:
\[
V'(r) = (2Au - B)\frac{\de u}{\de r}.
\]
Substitute $\de u/\de r$:
\[
V'(r) = -\frac{u}{\xi}(2A u - B).
\]
Equilibrium condition $V'(a)=0$ with $u_a\neq 0$:
\[
2A u_a - B = 0.
\]
Solve for $u_a$:
\[
u_a = \frac{B}{2A}.
\]
Invert $u_a = e^{-a/\xi}$:
\[
a = \xi\ln\frac{2A}{B}.
\]
Substitute numbers, $2A/B = 80$:
\[
a = (\SI{0.084}{nm})\ln 80.
\]
Evaluate $\ln 80 = 4.382$:
\[
\boxed{\;a = \SI{0.368}{nm}.\;}
\]

Binding energy: insert $u_a=B/(2A)$ into $V$:
\[
V(a) = A\left(\frac{B}{2A}\right)^2 - B\cdot\frac{B}{2A}.
\]
Simplify each term:
\[
V(a) = \frac{B^2}{4A} - \frac{B^2}{2A}.
\]
Combine:
\[
V(a) = -\frac{B^2}{4A}.
\]
Substitute numbers:
\[
\boxed{\;V(a) = -\frac{(40)^2}{4\cdot 1600}\,\si{eV} = \SI{-0.250}{eV}.\;}
\]

Second derivative. Differentiate $V'(r) = -(u/\xi)(2Au-B)$:
\[
V''(r) = -\frac{1}{\xi}\left[\frac{\de u}{\de r}(2Au-B) + 2Au\frac{\de u}{\de r}\right].
\]
Substitute $\de u/\de r=-u/\xi$:
\[
V''(r) = \frac{u}{\xi^2}(4Au - B).
\]
Evaluate at $u=u_a=B/(2A)$:
\[
\kappa \equiv V''(a) = \frac{B}{2A\xi^2}\bigl(4A\cdot\tfrac{B}{2A} - B\bigr).
\]
Simplify the bracket:
\[
\kappa = \frac{B}{2A\xi^2}\,B = \frac{B^2}{2A\xi^2}.
\]
Substitute numbers (in eV per nm$^2$):
\[
\kappa = \frac{40^2}{2\cdot 1600\cdot(0.084)^2}\,\si{eV\,nm^{-2}}.
\]
Evaluate:
\[
\kappa = 70.9\,\si{eV\,nm^{-2}}.
\]
Convert to SI via $1\,\si{eV\,nm^{-2}} = \SI{0.1602}{N\,m^{-1}}$:
\[
\boxed{\;\kappa \approx \SI{11.4}{N\,m^{-1}}.\;}
\]

\subsection*{(c) Sound velocity}

Monatomic chain dispersion:
\[
\omega(k) = 2\sqrt{\kappa/m}\,|\sin(ka/2)|.
\]
Long-wavelength group velocity:
\[
v_s = \frac{\de\omega}{\de k}\bigg|_{k\to 0} = 2\sqrt{\kappa/m}\cdot\frac{a}{2}.
\]
Simplify:
\[
v_s = a\sqrt{\kappa/m}.
\]
Atomic mass:
\[
m = \frac{M}{N_A} = \frac{\SI{0.207}{kg\,mol^{-1}}}{\SI{6.022e23}{mol^{-1}}}.
\]
Evaluate:
\[
m = \SI{3.44e-25}{kg}.
\]
Compute $\kappa/m$:
\[
\frac{\kappa}{m} = \frac{11.4}{3.44\times 10^{-25}}\,\si{s^{-2}} = \SI{3.31e25}{s^{-2}}.
\]
Take the square root:
\[
\sqrt{\kappa/m} = \SI{5.76e12}{s^{-1}}.
\]
Multiply by $a$:
\[
v_s = (\SI{0.368e-9}{m})\times(\SI{5.76e12}{s^{-1}}).
\]
Evaluate:
\[
\boxed{\;v_s \approx \SI{2.12e3}{m\,s^{-1}}.\;}
\]
Compare to measured Pb longitudinal speed $\sim\SI{2000}{m\,s^{-1}}$: agreement.

\subsection*{(d) Thermal expansion from cubic anharmonicity}

Expand $V$ to cubic order around $r=a$, with $x\equiv r-a$:
\[
V(r) \approx V(a) + \tfrac12\kappa\,x^2 + \tfrac16\kappa_3\,x^3,\qquad \kappa_3 \equiv V'''(a).
\]
Differentiate $V''$ once more:
\[
V'''(r) = -\frac{1}{\xi^3}(8A u^2 - B u).
\]
Insert $u_a=B/(2A)$:
\[
\kappa_3 = -\frac{1}{\xi^3}\left(\frac{2B^2}{A} - \frac{B^2}{2A}\right) = -\frac{3B^2}{2A\xi^3} < 0.
\]

Treat cubic term as perturbation; harmonic averages:
\[
\langle x\rangle_0 = 0,\quad \langle x^2\rangle_0 = \tfrac{k_B T}{\kappa},\quad \langle x^3\rangle_0 = 0,\quad \langle x^4\rangle_0 = 3\bigl(\tfrac{k_B T}{\kappa}\bigr)^2.
\]
First-order shift from the cubic term:
\[
\langle x\rangle \approx -\frac{\kappa_3}{6 k_B T}\langle x^4\rangle_0.
\]
Substitute $\langle x^4\rangle_0$:
\[
\langle x\rangle = -\frac{\kappa_3}{6 k_B T}\cdot 3\left(\frac{k_B T}{\kappa}\right)^2.
\]
Simplify:
\[
\langle x\rangle = -\frac{\kappa_3 k_B T}{2\kappa^2}.
\]
Differentiate w.r.t.\ $T$ and divide by $a$:
\[
\boxed{\;\alpha = \frac{1}{a}\frac{\de\langle r\rangle}{\de T} \sim \frac{|\kappa_3|\,k_B}{\kappa^2 a}.\;}
\]

Numerical sanity check: $|\kappa_3| = 3B^2/(2A\xi^3)$. Compute in eV/nm$^3$:
\[
|\kappa_3| = \frac{3\cdot 40^2}{2\cdot 1600\cdot(0.084)^3}\,\si{eV\,nm^{-3}} = 2.53\times 10^{3}\,\si{eV\,nm^{-3}}.
\]
Convert to SI (multiply by $1.602\times 10^{-19}/10^{-27} = 1.602\times 10^{8}$):
\[
|\kappa_3| \approx \SI{4.1e11}{J\,m^{-3}} = \SI{4.1e11}{N\,m^{-2}}.
\]
Substitute with $\kappa=\SI{11.4}{N\,m^{-1}}$, $a=\SI{0.368}{nm}$, $k_B = \SI{1.381e-23}{J\,K^{-1}}$:
\[
\alpha \sim \frac{(\SI{4.1e11}{N\,m^{-2}})(\SI{1.381e-23}{J\,K^{-1}})}{(\SI{11.4}{N\,m^{-1}})^2(\SI{3.68e-10}{m})}.
\]
Evaluate:
\[
\alpha \sim \SI{1.2e-4}{K^{-1}}.
\]
Order-of-magnitude correct (measured $\alpha_{\text{Pb}}\approx\SI{2.9e-5}{K^{-1}}$); positive sign follows because $\kappa_3<0$ widens the well outwards.

\subsection*{(e) Validity ranges}

\textbf{(i) Cubic-only expansion.} Require harmonic $\gg$ cubic energy at thermal amplitude $|x|\sim\sqrt{k_B T/\kappa}$:
\[
\tfrac12\kappa\,x^2 \gg \tfrac16|\kappa_3|\,x^3.
\]
Cancel one $x^2$ and reorganise:
\[
\kappa \gg \tfrac{1}{3}|\kappa_3|\,x.
\]
Substitute $|x|\sim\sqrt{k_BT/\kappa}$:
\[
\kappa \gg \frac{|\kappa_3|}{3}\sqrt{k_B T/\kappa}.
\]
Square and rearrange:
\[
k_B T \ll \frac{9\kappa^3}{\kappa_3^2}.
\]
Substitute numerical values:
\[
\frac{9\kappa^3}{\kappa_3^2} = \frac{9\cdot (11.4)^3}{(\SI{4.1e11}{})^2}\,\si{J} \sim \SI{8e-20}{J}.
\]
Divide by $k_B$:
\[
T \ll \SI{8e-20}{J}/(\SI{1.38e-23}{J\,K^{-1}}) \sim \SI{6e3}{K}.
\]
The physical bound is set well below this by the lead melting point $T_m\approx\SI{600}{K}$:
\[
\boxed{\;T \lesssim T_m \approx \SI{600}{K}.\;}
\]

\textbf{(ii) Quantum mechanics important.} Define crossover $k_BT_Q\sim\hbar\omega$ with $\omega = \sqrt{\kappa/m} = \SI{5.76e12}{s^{-1}}$:
\[
T_Q \sim \frac{\hbar\omega}{k_B}.
\]
Substitute numerical values:
\[
T_Q \sim \frac{(\SI{1.055e-34}{J\,s})(\SI{5.76e12}{s^{-1}})}{\SI{1.381e-23}{J\,K^{-1}}}.
\]
Evaluate:
\[
\boxed{\;T_Q \approx \SI{44}{K}.\;}
\]
For $T\lesssim T_Q$ zero-point motion dominates ($\langle x^2\rangle\to\hbar/(2m\omega)$), the harmonic equipartition $\langle x^2\rangle=k_BT/\kappa$ fails, the heat capacity drops below $k_B$/mode (Debye/Einstein freeze-out), and $\alpha\propto T^3$ at low $T$ (Gr\"uneisen).

The classical cubic-only treatment is therefore valid for $T_Q \lesssim T \lesssim T_m$, i.e.\ roughly $\SI{44}{K}\lesssim T\lesssim\SI{600}{K}$.

\section*{Question 4 --- Magnetism of GdN}

\textbf{Problem.} Define paramagnet, diamagnet, ferromagnet (with examples). Find $S$ of $\mathrm{Gd}^{3+}$ ($4f^7$) by Hund. Treat $\mathrm{Gd}^{3+}$ as Ising along $\hat z$ in FCC GdN ($a=\SI{0.50}{nm}$): compute Curie constant $C$ for $\chi=C/T$, modify $\chi$ for $T>T_c=\SI{70}{K}$, give $M(T=0)$, and sketch $M(T)$ for $0\le T<2T_c$ at $H_z=0$ and $H_z>0$.

\subsection*{(a) Definitions}

Paramagnet: $\chi>0$, no spontaneous order; high-$T$ Curie law $\chi\propto 1/T$. Example: MnSO$_4$ (or any rare-earth salt above its ordering $T$).

Diamagnet: $\chi<0$, repelled from field, Larmor response of closed shells. Example: Cu, Bi, water.

Ferromagnet: spontaneous magnetisation $M\neq 0$ below Curie $T_c$. Example: Fe, Ni, Co.

\subsection*{(b) Spin of Gd$^{3+}$}

Configuration $4f^7$: half-filled shell. Hund's first rule maximises $S$, all 7 spins parallel:
\[
\boxed{\;S = 7/2.\;}
\]
(Hund 2: $L=0$; Hund 3: $J=S=7/2$, $g_J = 2$.)

\subsection*{(c) Curie constant}

Ising along $\hat z$ with $S=7/2$ and $g=2$: each ion is a two-level system. Moment per ion:
\[
m = g\,S\,\mu_B = 2\cdot\tfrac72\,\mu_B.
\]
Simplify:
\[
m = 7\mu_B.
\]
Zeeman energies in field $B=\mu_0 H_z$ with $m_z=\pm m$:
\[
E_\pm = \mp m B.
\]
Two-level partition function:
\[
Z = e^{\beta m B}+e^{-\beta m B} = 2\cosh(\beta m B).
\]
Thermal-average moment:
\[
\langle m_z\rangle = m\tanh(\beta m B).
\]
Linearise for $\beta m B\ll 1$ using $\tanh x\approx x$:
\[
\langle m_z\rangle \approx \beta m^2 B = \frac{m^2 \mu_0 H_z}{k_B T}.
\]
Multiply by number density:
\[
M_z = n\langle m_z\rangle = \frac{n m^2 \mu_0}{k_B T} H_z.
\]
Read off $\chi=M_z/H_z$ and $C$:
\[
\chi = \frac{n m^2 \mu_0}{k_B T} = \frac{C}{T},\qquad C = \frac{n m^2 \mu_0}{k_B}.
\]

Number density of Gd$^{3+}$: FCC conventional cell contains 4 formula units:
\[
n = \frac{4}{a^3} = \frac{4}{(\SI{0.50e-9}{m})^3}.
\]
Evaluate:
\[
n = \SI{3.20e28}{m^{-3}}.
\]
Compute $(7\mu_B)^2$ with $\mu_B = \SI{9.274e-24}{J\,T^{-1}}$:
\[
m^2 = (\SI{6.492e-23}{J\,T^{-1}})^2 = \SI{4.215e-45}{J^2\,T^{-2}}.
\]
Substitute numerical values into $C$ (with $\mu_0 = \SI{1.257e-6}{T\,m\,A^{-1}}$):
\[
C = \frac{(\SI{3.20e28}{m^{-3}})(\SI{4.215e-45}{J^2\,T^{-2}})(\SI{1.257e-6}{T\,m\,A^{-1}})}{\SI{1.381e-23}{J\,K^{-1}}}.
\]
Evaluate the numerator:
\[
n m^2 \mu_0 = \SI{1.696e-22}{J}.
\]
Divide by $k_B$:
\[
\boxed{\;C \approx \SI{12.3}{K}.\;}
\]

\subsection*{(d) Curie--Weiss modification and $M(0)$}

Mean-field treatment of exchange: replace $T$ by $T-T_c$:
\[
\boxed{\;\chi(T) = \frac{C}{T - T_c}\quad\text{for }T > T_c.\;}
\]
Denominator vanishes at $T_c$, signalling spontaneous order.

At $T=0$ all Ising spins align with the field:
\[
M(0) = n m = 7 n \mu_B.
\]
Substitute numbers:
\[
M(0) = (\SI{3.20e28}{m^{-3}})\times 7\times(\SI{9.274e-24}{J\,T^{-1}}).
\]
Evaluate:
\[
\boxed{\;M(0) \approx \SI{2.08e6}{A\,m^{-1}}.\;}
\]

\end{itemize}
