\section*{Question 1 --- 4-vectors, kinematics, dynamics}

\subsection*{(a) Intervals, null separation, orthogonality}

\textbf{Problem.} Conditions for spacelike/timelike intervals between events $D=(ct_d,\bm x_d)$, $G=(ct_g,\bm x_g)$. Null separation? Conditions $Y^\mu X_\mu=0$. Which pairs of $A,K,J$ are orthogonal?

Define the interval 4-vector:
\[ \Delta X^\mu = D^\mu - G^\mu = (c\Delta t,\Delta\bm x). \]
Identify the components:
\[ \Delta t = t_d-t_g,\qquad \Delta\bm x=\bm x_d-\bm x_g. \]
Form the Minkowski-square in signature $(+,-,-,-)$:
\[ \Delta X^\mu \Delta X_\mu = c^2\Delta t^2 - |\Delta\bm x|^2. \]
State the timelike condition (causally connected by sub-light signal):
\[ \boxed{\;c^2\Delta t^2 - |\Delta\bm x|^2 > 0\;} \]
State the spacelike condition (no causal signal possible):
\[ \boxed{\;c^2\Delta t^2 - |\Delta\bm x|^2 < 0\;} \]
State the null (light-like) condition:
\[ \boxed{\;c^2\Delta t^2 - |\Delta\bm x|^2 = 0\;} \]

\noindent Yes, two events can be connected by a null vector --- a photon emitted at $G$ and absorbed at $D$ traces such a null worldline.

\noindent\textbf{When is $Y^\mu X_\mu=0$?} Three independent cases:
\begin{enumerate}
\item One of the 4-vectors vanishes: $Y^\mu=0$ or $X^\mu=0$.
\item Both are null and parallel: $Y^\mu Y_\mu = X^\mu X_\mu = 0$ with $Y^\mu \propto X^\mu$.
\item One is timelike (or null) and the other spacelike (or null), oriented so the spatial overlap cancels the temporal product --- i.e.\ a genuinely orthogonal Minkowski pair.
\end{enumerate}
A timelike 4-vector cannot be orthogonal to another timelike 4-vector (their inner product retains the sign of $c^2\Delta t^2$).

\noindent\textbf{Pairs among $A,K,J$.}

Start from the normalization $U^\mu U_\mu=c^2$:
\[ U^\mu U_\mu = c^2. \]
Differentiate with respect to $\tau$:
\[ 2\,A^\mu U_\mu = 0. \]
Conclude:
\[ A^\mu U_\mu = 0. \]
Write $J^\mu$ for a particle of rest charge density $\rho_0$:
\[ J^\mu = \rho_0 U^\mu. \]
Contract with $A_\mu$:
\[ A^\mu J_\mu = \rho_0\, A^\mu U_\mu = 0. \]
\[ \boxed{\;A\cdot J = 0\ \text{always}.\;} \]

\noindent For $K$ (null) and $A$ (spacelike): no identity forces $A\cdot K=0$; generically $A^\mu K_\mu\neq 0$. Likewise $K\cdot J = \rho_0 K^\mu U_\mu \propto \omega_\text{rest}\neq 0$ in general (the photon frequency seen by the charge). So only $A\cdot J=0$ is guaranteed.

\subsection*{(b) 4-momentum, 4-acceleration, parallel case}

\textbf{Problem.} Define $\bm P$, $\bm A$. Compute $P^\mu P_\mu$. Show $A^\mu A_\mu=\gamma^6 a^2$ when $\bm v\parallel\bm a$.

Define 4-momentum:
\[ P^\mu = m U^\mu = (\gamma mc,\gamma m\bm v). \]
Define 4-acceleration:
\[ A^\mu = \dv{U^\mu}{\tau} = \gamma\dv{U^\mu}{t}. \]
Form the Minkowski-square of $P^\mu$:
\[ P^\mu P_\mu = (\gamma mc)^2 - (\gamma m\bm v)\cdot(\gamma m\bm v). \]
Expand:
\[ P^\mu P_\mu = \gamma^2 m^2 c^2 - \gamma^2 m^2 v^2. \]
Factor $\gamma^2 m^2$:
\[ P^\mu P_\mu = \gamma^2 m^2 (c^2-v^2). \]
Pull out $c^2$:
\[ P^\mu P_\mu = \gamma^2 m^2 c^2(1-v^2/c^2). \]
Apply $\gamma^2(1-v^2/c^2)=1$:
\[ \boxed{\;P^\mu P_\mu = m^2 c^2.\;} \]

\noindent\textbf{4-acceleration with $\bm v\parallel\bm a$.} Write 4-velocity components:
\[ U^0=\gamma c,\qquad \bm U=\gamma\bm v. \]
Differentiate the time component with respect to $t$:
\[ \dv{U^0}{t} = c\,\dot\gamma. \]
Differentiate the spatial component:
\[ \dv{\bm U}{t} = \dot\gamma\,\bm v + \gamma\,\bm a. \]
Differentiate $\gamma=(1-v^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$ via the chain rule:
\[ \dot\gamma = -\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(1-v^2/c^2\bigr)^{-3/2}\!\bigl(-2\bm v\!\cdot\!\bm a/c^2\bigr). \]
Simplify:
\[ \dot\gamma = \gamma^3 \frac{\bm v\!\cdot\!\bm a}{c^2}. \]
Apply $\bm v\parallel\bm a\Rightarrow \bm v\!\cdot\!\bm a = va$:
\[ \dot\gamma = \gamma^3 \frac{va}{c^2}. \]
Apply $A^\mu = \gamma\,dU^\mu/dt$ to the time component:
\[ A^0 = \gamma\,\dot\gamma\, c. \]
Substitute $\dot\gamma$:
\[ A^0 = \gamma^4\frac{va}{c}. \]
Apply $A^\mu = \gamma\,dU^\mu/dt$ to the spatial component:
\[ \bm A = \gamma\bigl(\dot\gamma\bm v + \gamma\bm a\bigr). \]
Substitute $\dot\gamma$ and $\bm v = v\hat{\bm a}$:
\[ \bm A = \gamma^4\frac{v^2 a}{c^2}\hat{\bm a} + \gamma^2 a\hat{\bm a}. \]
Factor $\gamma^2 a\,\hat{\bm a}$:
\[ \bm A = \gamma^2 a\Bigl(\gamma^2\frac{v^2}{c^2}+1\Bigr)\hat{\bm a}. \]
Apply $1+\gamma^2 v^2/c^2 = \gamma^2$:
\[ \bm A = \gamma^4 a\,\hat{\bm a}. \]
Take the magnitude:
\[ |\bm A|=\gamma^4 a. \]
Form the Minkowski-square:
\[ A^\mu A_\mu = (A^0)^2 - |\bm A|^2. \]
Substitute:
\[ A^\mu A_\mu = \gamma^8\frac{v^2 a^2}{c^2} - \gamma^8 a^2. \]
Factor $\gamma^8 a^2$:
\[ A^\mu A_\mu = \gamma^8 a^2\Bigl(\frac{v^2}{c^2}-1\Bigr). \]
Apply $v^2/c^2 - 1 = -1/\gamma^2$:
\[ A^\mu A_\mu = -\gamma^8 a^2/\gamma^2. \]
Simplify:
\[ A^\mu A_\mu = -\gamma^6 a^2. \]
In signature $(+,-,-,-)$ the spacelike 4-acceleration has negative invariant; the question's $\gamma^6 a^2$ is the magnitude (equivalent to $(-,+,+,+)$ convention):
\[ \boxed{\;|A^\mu A_\mu| = \gamma^6 a^2.\;} \]

\subsection*{(c) Inverse-square central force: energy conservation}

\textbf{Problem.} For $\bm f = -\alpha\bm r/r^3$ (attractive, $\alpha>0$), derive $\gamma mc^2 - \alpha/r = \text{const}$.

Start with the relativistic 2nd law (rest mass preserved):
\[ \dv{t}(\gamma m\bm v) = \bm f. \]
Dot with $\bm v$:
\[ \bm v\!\cdot\!\dv{t}(\gamma m\bm v) = \bm f\!\cdot\!\bm v. \]
Recognise the relativistic energy equation:
\[ \dv{t}(\gamma mc^2) = \bm f\!\cdot\!\bm v. \]
Insert the attractive inverse-square force $\bm f = -\alpha\bm r/r^3$:
\[ \bm f\!\cdot\!\bm v = -\frac{\alpha}{r^3}\,\bm r\!\cdot\!\bm v. \]
Use $\bm r\!\cdot\!\bm v = r\dot r$:
\[ \bm f\!\cdot\!\bm v = -\frac{\alpha\dot r}{r^2}. \]
Use $\dv{}{t}(1/r) = -\dot r/r^2$:
\[ -\frac{\alpha\dot r}{r^2} = \alpha\dv{t}\!\Bigl(\frac{1}{r}\Bigr) = \dv{t}\!\Bigl(\frac{\alpha}{r}\Bigr). \]
Combine:
\[ \dv{t}(\gamma mc^2) = \dv{t}\!\Bigl(\frac{\alpha}{r}\Bigr). \]
Collect into a single time derivative:
\[ \dv{t}\!\Bigl(\gamma mc^2 - \frac{\alpha}{r}\Bigr) = 0. \]
\[ \boxed{\;\gamma mc^2 - \frac{\alpha}{r} = \text{const}.\;} \]
\textit{Example.} Electron-proton Coulomb attraction with $\alpha = e^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0)$, or Newtonian gravity with $\alpha = GMm$.

\subsection*{(d) Electron accelerated across a 5 m gap, $E=\SI{10}{MV/m}$}

\textbf{Problem.} Find $\gamma$ at exit, time of flight, sketch $\gamma(t),\beta(t)$, $\Delta\beta$ if field reduced 20\%, and proton case.

Apply the work-energy theorem:
\[ \gamma mc^2 = mc^2 + eEL. \]
Solve for $\gamma$:
\[ \gamma = 1 + \frac{eEL}{mc^2}. \]
Compute $eEL$ with $E=\SI{10}{MV/m}$, $L=\SI{5}{m}$:
\[ eEL = \SI{10}{MeV/m}\times\SI{5}{m}=\SI{50}{MeV}. \]
Insert $mc^2=\SI{0.511}{MeV}$:
\[ \gamma = 1 + \frac{50}{0.511}. \]
Evaluate the ratio:
\[ \gamma = 1 + 97.85. \]
\[ \boxed{\;\gamma\approx 98.85\;} \]

\noindent\textbf{Time of flight.} Write the 1-D equation of motion (constant force):
\[ \dv{t}(\gamma m v) = eE. \]
Integrate from rest:
\[ \gamma m v = eEt. \]
Square and apply $\gamma^2 v^2 = c^2(\gamma^2-1)$:
\[ m^2 c^2(\gamma^2-1) = (eEt)^2. \]
Solve for $\gamma(t)$:
\[ \gamma(t) = \sqrt{1+\Bigl(\frac{eEt}{mc}\Bigr)^2}. \]
Integrate $\dv*{x}{t}=v=c\sqrt{1-1/\gamma^2}$:
\[ x(t) = \frac{mc^2}{eE}\Bigl[\sqrt{1+(eEt/mc)^2}-1\Bigr]. \]
Set $x=L$:
\[ \sqrt{1+(eEt/mc)^2} = 1 + \frac{eEL}{mc^2} = \gamma. \]
Square:
\[ \Bigl(\frac{eEt}{mc}\Bigr)^2 = \gamma^2-1. \]
Solve for $t$:
\[ t = \frac{mc}{eE}\sqrt{\gamma^2-1}. \]
Compute $\sqrt{\gamma^2-1}$:
\[ \sqrt{\gamma^2-1} = \sqrt{98.85^2-1}. \]
Evaluate:
\[ \sqrt{\gamma^2-1} = \sqrt{9770.32} \approx 98.84. \]
Compute $mc^2/(eE)$:
\[ \frac{mc^2}{eE} = \frac{\SI{0.511}{MeV}}{\SI{10}{MV/m}}. \]
Reduce the units:
\[ \frac{mc^2}{eE} = \SI{0.0511}{m}. \]
Divide by $c$:
\[ \frac{mc}{eE} = \frac{\SI{0.0511}{m}}{c}. \]
Substitute into $t = (mc/eE)\sqrt{\gamma^2-1}$:
\[ t = \frac{\SI{0.0511}{m}}{c}\times 98.84. \]
Multiply:
\[ t = \frac{\SI{5.05}{m}}{c}. \]
Insert $c=\SI{2.998e8}{m/s}$:
\[ t = \frac{5.05}{2.998\times 10^8}\,\text{s}. \]
\[ \boxed{\;t \approx \SI{1.69e-8}{s}\approx \SI{16.9}{ns}.\;} \]

\noindent\textbf{Field reduced by 20\% ($E\to 0.8E$).} Recompute $\gamma'$:
\[ \gamma' = 1 + \frac{e(0.8E)L}{m_ec^2}. \]
Substitute $eEL/(m_ec^2) = 97.85$:
\[ \gamma' = 1 + 0.8\times 97.85 = 1 + 78.28. \]
Evaluate:
\[ \gamma' = 79.28. \]
Both $\beta,\beta'$ are very close to $1$; expand $\beta = \sqrt{1-1/\gamma^2}\simeq 1 - 1/(2\gamma^2)$:
\[ 1-\beta \simeq \frac{1}{2\gamma^2}. \]
Substitute $\gamma=98.85$:
\[ 1-\beta = \frac{1}{2\times 98.85^2} = \frac{1}{19543}. \]
Evaluate:
\[ 1-\beta \approx \SI{5.12e-5}{}. \]
Substitute $\gamma'=79.28$:
\[ 1-\beta' = \frac{1}{2\times 79.28^2} = \frac{1}{12571}. \]
Evaluate:
\[ 1-\beta' \approx \SI{7.96e-5}{}. \]
Form the difference using $\beta'-\beta = (1-\beta) - (1-\beta')$:
\[ \Delta\beta = \SI{5.12e-5}{}-\SI{7.96e-5}{}. \]
\[ \boxed{\;\Delta\beta \approx -\SI{2.8e-5}{}\ \text{(decrease)}.\;} \]

\noindent\textbf{Proton instead.} With $m_p c^2 = \SI{938.3}{MeV}\gg \SI{50}{MeV}$, motion is mildly relativistic. Compute $\gamma_p$:
\[ \gamma_p = 1 + \frac{eEL}{m_p c^2} = 1 + \frac{50}{938.3}. \]
Evaluate the ratio:
\[ \gamma_p = 1 + 0.0533 = 1.0533. \]
Compute $\gamma_p^2$:
\[ \gamma_p^2 = 1.0533^2 = 1.1094. \]
Form $1/\gamma_p^2$:
\[ 1/\gamma_p^2 = 1/1.1094 = 0.9014. \]
Apply $\beta_p = \sqrt{1-1/\gamma_p^2}$:
\[ \beta_p = \sqrt{1-0.9014} = \sqrt{0.0986}. \]
Evaluate:
\[ \beta_p = 0.314. \]
\[ \boxed{\;\gamma_p\approx 1.05,\ \beta_p\approx 0.31.\;} \]
Hence the proton is far less relativistic ($\gamma_p\ll\gamma_e$, $\beta_p\ll 1$).

\section*{Question 2 --- Photons, decays, fields, mirror reflection}

\subsection*{(a) Photon 4-wavevector, phase invariance}

\textbf{Problem.} Components of $\bm K$, dispersion, $v_\text{ph}$ and $v_g$, show $\phi$ is Lorentz-invariant; is $v_\text{ph}$ invariant?

Write the 4-wavevector:
\[ K^\mu = (\omega/c,\bm k). \]
Apply vacuum dispersion:
\[ \omega = c|\bm k|. \]
Form the null condition:
\[ K^\mu K_\mu = \omega^2/c^2 - |\bm k|^2 = 0. \]
Define the phase velocity along $\hat{\bm k}$:
\[ v_\text{ph} = \omega/|\bm k| = c. \]
Define the group velocity:
\[ \bm v_g = \nabla_{\bm k}\omega = c\hat{\bm k},\qquad |\bm v_g|=c. \]
Write the phase of a plane wave:
\[ \phi(\bm r,t) = \bm k\!\cdot\!\bm r - \omega t. \]
Identify the 4-vector contraction:
\[ \phi = -K^\mu X_\mu. \]
A contraction of two 4-vectors is a Lorentz scalar:
\[ \boxed{\;\phi = -K^\mu X_\mu \text{ is Lorentz-invariant.}\;} \]

\noindent\textbf{Is $v_\text{ph}$ Lorentz-invariant?} For a vacuum photon, $\omega'/|\bm k'|=c$ in every frame, so yes --- but only because $c$ is the universal speed limit. In a medium with $v_\text{ph}\neq c$, a Lorentz boost generally changes both $\omega$ and $|\bm k|$ such that $v_\text{ph}$ is \emph{not} invariant. So:
\[ \boxed{\;v_\text{ph}=c\text{ in vacuum: invariant. In a medium: not invariant.}\;} \]

\subsection*{(b) Forbidden and allowed photon processes}

\textbf{Problem.} Can $\gamma\to m+\gamma'$? Photon-photon $\to e^+e^-$: minimum number, threshold energy.

\textbf{Decay $\gamma\to m+\gamma'$.} Write 4-momentum conservation using $\hbar K$ for photon 4-momenta:
\[ \hbar K_1^\mu = P_m^\mu + \hbar K_2^\mu. \]
Isolate $P_m^\mu$:
\[ P_m^\mu = \hbar K_1^\mu - \hbar K_2^\mu. \]
Square both sides:
\[ P_m^\mu P_{m\mu} = \hbar^2 K_1^\mu K_{1\mu} - 2\hbar^2 K_1^\mu K_{2\mu} + \hbar^2 K_2^\mu K_{2\mu}. \]
Apply $K_1^\mu K_{1\mu} = K_2^\mu K_{2\mu} = 0$ and $P_m^\mu P_{m\mu} = m^2c^2$:
\[ m^2c^2 = -2\hbar^2 K_1^\mu K_{2\mu}. \]
Evaluate $K_1\cdot K_2$ with $\theta_{12}$ the angle between the photons:
\[ K_1^\mu K_{2\mu} = \omega_1\omega_2/c^2 - \bm k_1\!\cdot\!\bm k_2. \]
Substitute $\bm k_1\!\cdot\!\bm k_2 = (\omega_1\omega_2/c^2)\cos\theta_{12}$:
\[ K_1^\mu K_{2\mu} = (\omega_1\omega_2/c^2)(1-\cos\theta_{12}). \]
Insert into the squared equation:
\[ m^2c^2 = -2\hbar^2(\omega_1\omega_2/c^2)(1-\cos\theta_{12}). \]
Observe: LHS $\ge 0$, RHS $\le 0$ since $1-\cos\theta_{12}\ge 0$ and $\omega_i>0$. Equality requires $m=0$ and $\theta_{12}=0$ (trivial).
\[ \boxed{\;\text{Forbidden: a single photon cannot decay into }m+\gamma.\;} \]

\noindent\textbf{Photon-photon $\to e^+e^-$.} A single photon has $K^\mu K_\mu=0$ and cannot create a massive system ($P_{\text{pair}}^2=(2m_e c)^2>0$) by itself. Two photons suffice if they are not collinear:
\[ \hbar^2(K_1+K_2)^\mu (K_1+K_2)_\mu = (2m_e c)^2. \]
\[ \boxed{\;\text{Minimum: }2\text{ photons.}\;} \]

\noindent\textbf{Threshold.} Equal frequencies, head-on collision: $\bm k_2 = -\bm k_1$ with $|\bm k_1|=|\bm k_2|=\omega/c$. Compute $K_1\!\cdot\!K_2$:
\[ K_1^\mu K_{2\mu} = \omega^2/c^2 - \bm k_1\!\cdot\!\bm k_2. \]
Insert $\bm k_1\!\cdot\!\bm k_2 = -\omega^2/c^2$:
\[ K_1^\mu K_{2\mu} = \omega^2/c^2 + \omega^2/c^2 = 2\omega^2/c^2. \]
Expand $(K_1+K_2)^2$:
\[ (K_1+K_2)^\mu (K_1+K_2)_\mu = 2 K_1^\mu K_{2\mu} = 4\omega^2/c^2. \]
Threshold ($e^+e^-$ produced at rest in CM):
\[ \hbar^2\cdot 4\omega^2/c^2 = (2m_e c)^2. \]
Solve for $\omega$:
\[ \hbar\omega = m_e c^2. \]
Photon energy at threshold:
\[ E_\gamma = \hbar\omega = m_e c^2. \]
\[ \boxed{\;E_\gamma^\text{min} = m_e c^2 \approx \SI{0.511}{MeV}\text{ per photon.}\;} \]

\subsection*{(c) Conserved 4-momentum components in $\bm E$ or $\bm B$ field}

\textbf{Problem.} Electron with $\bm v_0=(v_0,0,0)$. Which $P^\mu$ are conserved for (i) $\bm E=(E_x,0,0)$, (ii) $\bm B=(0,B_y,0)$.

Lorentz force on electron (charge $q=-e$):
\[ \dv{\bm p}{t} = -e(\bm E + \bm v\times\bm B). \]
Energy rate:
\[ \dv{E_\text{tot}}{t} = -e\bm E\!\cdot\!\bm v. \]

\textbf{(i) $\bm E=(E_x,0,0)$, no $\bm B$.} Spatial force components:
\[ \dv{p_x}{t} = -eE_x,\qquad \dv{p_y}{t}=0,\qquad \dv{p_z}{t}=0. \]
Energy rate evaluated:
\[ \dv{E_\text{tot}}{t} = -eE_xv_x\neq 0. \]
Identify the conserved components:
\[ \boxed{\;P^y,\, P^z\text{ conserved}; P^0,\,P^x\text{ not.}\;} \]
Initial conditions give $p_y=p_z=0$ throughout.

\textbf{(ii) $\bm B=(0,B_y,0)$, no $\bm E$.} Magnetic force does no work:
\[ \dv{E_\text{tot}}{t}=0\ \Rightarrow\ P^0\text{ conserved.} \]
Check the spatial force has no $y$-component. Compute $\bm v\times\bm B$ for general $\bm v=(v_x,v_y,v_z)$:
\[ \bm v\times\bm B = (v_x,v_y,v_z)\times(0,B_y,0) = (-v_z B_y,\,0,\,v_x B_y). \]
The $y$-component vanishes identically, so:
\[ \dv{p_y}{t} = 0. \]
With $p_y(0)=0$, $p_y$ remains zero. $P^x$ and $P^z$ rotate into each other (circular motion in $xz$-plane):
\[ \boxed{\;P^0\text{ and }P^y\text{ conserved}; P^x,P^z\text{ rotate (circular motion).}\;} \]

\subsection*{(d) Reflection from a relativistic mirror}

\textbf{Problem.} Mirror moves at $\beta_x=0.99$ along $\hat{\bm x}$. Lab photon $\lambda_1=\SI{1}{\mu m}$. In mirror frame $\bm k'=(-k'_x,k'_y,0)$ with $|k'_x|=|k'_y|$. Find lab reflection angle and reflected $\lambda_2$.

\noindent\textbf{Step 1: Lorentz factor.} Compute $1-\beta^2$:
\[ 1-\beta^2 = 1-0.99^2 = 1-0.9801 = 0.0199. \]
Take inverse square root:
\[ \gamma = 1/\sqrt{0.0199} = 7.0888. \]

\noindent\textbf{Step 2: Lab incident $\bm K_1$.} In mirror frame, $|k'_x|=|k'_y|$ with $k'_x<0$ (photon moves in $-\hat{\bm x}$ direction in the mirror frame). Write the 4-wavevector:
\[ K_1'^\mu = (\omega'/c)\,(1,-1/\sqrt{2},1/\sqrt{2},0),\qquad \omega' = c|k'_x|\sqrt{2}. \]
Boost from $S'\to S$ uses relative velocity $+v$ (the mirror frame moves at $+v\hat{\bm x}$ relative to lab):
\[ \omega_1/c = \gamma(\omega'/c + \beta k'_{1x}). \]
Substitute $k'_{1x}=-(\omega'/c)/\sqrt 2$:
\[ \omega_1/c = \gamma(\omega'/c)(1-\beta/\sqrt{2}). \]
Boost $k_x$ component similarly:
\[ k_{1x} = \gamma(k'_{1x} + \beta\omega'/c) = \gamma(\omega'/c)(\beta - 1/\sqrt{2}). \]
Transverse components unchanged:
\[ k_{1y} = k'_{1y} = (\omega'/c)/\sqrt{2}. \]
Compute $\beta/\sqrt 2$ with $\beta=0.99$:
\[ \beta/\sqrt 2 = 0.99/1.4142 = 0.7000. \]
Insert into $\omega_1/c$:
\[ \omega_1/c = 7.0888\,(\omega'/c)(1-0.7000). \]
Multiply through:
\[ \omega_1/c = 7.0888\times 0.3000\,(\omega'/c) = 2.127\,\omega'/c. \]
Invert to express $\omega'$ in terms of given $\omega_1$:
\[ \omega' = \omega_1/2.127. \]

\noindent\textbf{Step 3: Reflection in mirror frame.} A perfect mirror flips the normal component of $\bm k'$:
\[ K_2'^\mu = (\omega'/c)\,(1,+1/\sqrt 2,1/\sqrt 2,0). \]
Reflection angle in mirror frame is $45^\circ$ from normal.

\noindent\textbf{Step 4: Boost reflected photon back to lab.} Apply $S'\to S$ Lorentz transform:
\[ \omega_2/c = \gamma(\omega'/c + \beta k'_{2x}). \]
Substitute $k'_{2x}=+(\omega'/c)/\sqrt 2$:
\[ \omega_2/c = \gamma(\omega'/c)(1+\beta/\sqrt 2). \]
Boost the spatial component:
\[ k_{2x} = \gamma(k'_{2x} + \beta\omega'/c) = \gamma(\omega'/c)(1/\sqrt 2 + \beta). \]
Transverse component unchanged:
\[ k_{2y} = k'_{2y} = (\omega'/c)/\sqrt 2. \]
Insert $\beta/\sqrt 2 = 0.7000$ into $\omega_2/c$:
\[ \omega_2/c = 7.0888\,(\omega'/c)(1+0.7000). \]
Multiply through:
\[ \omega_2/c = 7.0888\times 1.7000\,(\omega'/c) = 12.051\,\omega'/c. \]
Form the frequency ratio:
\[ \omega_2/\omega_1 = 12.051/2.127 = 5.666. \]
Use $\lambda \propto 1/\omega$:
\[ \lambda_2 = \lambda_1\,\omega_1/\omega_2 = \SI{1}{\mu m}/5.666. \]
\[ \boxed{\;\lambda_2 \approx \SI{0.177}{\mu m} \approx \SI{177}{nm}.\;} \]

\noindent\textbf{Reflection angle in lab.} Measure from $+\hat{\bm x}$ (mirror normal direction):
\[ \tan\theta_2 = k_{2y}/k_{2x} = \frac{(\omega'/c)/\sqrt 2}{\gamma(\omega'/c)(1/\sqrt 2+\beta)}. \]
Cancel $\omega'/c$:
\[ \tan\theta_2 = \frac{1/\sqrt 2}{\gamma(1/\sqrt 2+\beta)} = \frac{0.7071}{7.0888\times 1.6971}. \]
Compute denominator:
\[ 7.0888\times 1.6971 = 12.030. \]
Evaluate:
\[ \tan\theta_2 = 0.7071/12.030 = 0.05878. \]
\[ \boxed{\;\theta_2 = \arctan(0.0588) \approx 3.36^\circ\;} \]
The reflected photon emerges almost along $+\hat{\bm x}$ (relativistic beaming towards mirror motion direction).

\section*{Question 3 --- Bounds, Compton, periodic-field undulator}

\subsection*{(a) Bounds on forces, $v_\text{ph}$; Compton shift}

\textbf{Problem.} Bounds on forces/accelerations? On phase velocity? Derive Compton shift.

\textbf{No upper bound on force or acceleration.} Special relativity bounds the \emph{velocity} of a massive particle ($v<c$); $\bm a = \de\bm v/\de t$ can be arbitrarily large transiently (only the integrated speed-gain is constrained). Similarly $\bm f = \de\bm p/\de t$ is unbounded since $|\bm p|=\gamma m|\bm v|\to\infty$ as $v\to c$.

\noindent\textbf{Phase velocity is unbounded.} In vacuum $v_\text{ph}=c$ exactly. In a medium $v_\text{ph}=\omega/|\bm k|$ can exceed $c$ (e.g.\ X-rays in matter, plasmas above $\omega_p$). Special relativity bounds only the group velocity / signal speed, not the phase velocity.

\noindent\textbf{Compton shift.} Write 4-momenta: photon $\hbar K = (\hbar\omega/c)(1,\hat{\bm n})$, electron at rest $P_e=(m_e c,\bm 0)$; after scatter $\hbar K'=(\hbar\omega'/c)(1,\hat{\bm n}')$, electron $P_e'$. 4-momentum conservation:
\[ \hbar K + P_e = \hbar K' + P_e'. \]
Isolate $P_e'$:
\[ P_e' = P_e + \hbar K - \hbar K'. \]
Square both sides:
\[ P_e'^\mu P_{e\mu}' = (P_e + \hbar K - \hbar K')^\mu (P_e+\hbar K-\hbar K')_\mu. \]
Expand:
\[ P_e'^2 = P_e^2 + \hbar^2 K^2 + \hbar^2 K'^2 + 2\hbar P_e\!\cdot\!K - 2\hbar P_e\!\cdot\!K' - 2\hbar^2 K\!\cdot\!K'. \]
Apply $P_e^2 = P_e'^2 = m_e^2 c^2$ and $K^2=K'^2=0$:
\[ m_e^2 c^2 = m_e^2 c^2 + 2\hbar P_e\!\cdot\!K - 2\hbar P_e\!\cdot\!K' - 2\hbar^2 K\!\cdot\!K'. \]
Cancel $m_e^2 c^2$ and divide by $2\hbar$:
\[ P_e\!\cdot\!(K-K') = \hbar\, K\!\cdot\!K'. \]
Evaluate in rest frame $P_e=(m_e c,\bm 0)$:
\[ P_e\!\cdot\!K = m_e c\cdot \omega/c = m_e\omega. \]
Similarly:
\[ P_e\!\cdot\!K' = m_e\omega'. \]
Compute $K\!\cdot\!K'$ with $\hat{\bm n}\!\cdot\!\hat{\bm n}'=\cos\theta$:
\[ K\!\cdot\!K' = \omega\omega'/c^2 - (\omega\omega'/c^2)\cos\theta. \]
Factor:
\[ K\!\cdot\!K' = (\omega\omega'/c^2)(1-\cos\theta). \]
Substitute into the conservation identity:
\[ m_e(\omega-\omega') = \hbar(\omega\omega'/c^2)(1-\cos\theta). \]
Divide by $m_e\omega\omega'$:
\[ \frac{1}{\omega'}-\frac{1}{\omega} = \frac{\hbar(1-\cos\theta)}{m_e c^2}. \]
Use $\lambda = 2\pi c/\omega$ so $1/\omega = \lambda/(2\pi c)$:
\[ \frac{\lambda'-\lambda}{2\pi c} = \frac{\hbar(1-\cos\theta)}{m_e c^2}. \]
Solve for $\lambda'-\lambda$:
\[ \lambda'-\lambda = \frac{2\pi\hbar}{m_e c}(1-\cos\theta). \]
Use $h = 2\pi\hbar$:
\[ \boxed{\;\lambda' - \lambda = \frac{h}{m_e c}(1-\cos\theta) = \lambda_C(1-\cos\theta)\;} \]
with $\lambda_C = h/(m_e c) \approx \SI{2.43}{pm}$.

\subsection*{(b) Distance between two photons in $S'$}

\textbf{Problem.} Two photons separated by $x_0$ along $\hat{\bm x}$ in $S$. Find separation in $S'$ moving with $\bm v=(v_x,0,0)$.

Take both photons propagating along $+\hat{\bm x}$. In $S$ at $t=0$ the two emission/observation events are
\[ E_A=(0,0,0,0),\qquad E_B=(0,x_0,0,0). \]
Apply the Lorentz boost along $+x$ at velocity $v$:
\[ x'=\gamma(x-vt),\qquad t'=\gamma(t-vx/c^2). \]
Transform $E_A$:
\[ E_A'=(0,0,0,0). \]
Transform $E_B$ with $t=0$, $x=x_0$:
\[ x'_B = \gamma x_0. \]
\[ t'_B = -\gamma v x_0/c^2. \]
The events are not simultaneous in $S'$. Each photon's null worldline in $S'$ is $x'=a_i + ct'$; solve for $a_B$:
\[ a_B = x'_B - c t'_B. \]
Substitute:
\[ a_B = \gamma x_0 - c(-\gamma v x_0/c^2). \]
Simplify:
\[ a_B = \gamma x_0 + \gamma v x_0/c. \]
Factor $\gamma x_0$:
\[ a_B = \gamma x_0(1+\beta). \]
Photon A passes through the origin, so $a_A=0$. The separation at fixed $t'$ is
\[ \Delta x' = a_B - a_A = \gamma x_0(1+\beta). \]
Rewrite $\gamma = 1/\sqrt{(1-\beta)(1+\beta)}$:
\[ \gamma(1+\beta) = \frac{1+\beta}{\sqrt{(1-\beta)(1+\beta)}} = \sqrt{\frac{1+\beta}{1-\beta}}. \]
\[ \boxed{\;\Delta x' = x_0\,\gamma(1+\beta) = x_0\sqrt{\frac{1+\beta}{1-\beta}}.\;} \]
This is the relativistic Doppler factor for a separation along the propagation direction (photons ``stretched apart'' when $S'$ recedes).

\subsection*{(c) Head-on Compton: $\lambda_2 \approx \lambda_1/(4\gamma^2)$}

\textbf{Problem.} Electron and photon counter-propagate; head-on backscatter. Show $\lambda_2 \approx \lambda_1/(4\gamma^2)$ when $\hbar\omega\ll m_e c^2$. Numerical for $\beta=0.999$, $\lambda_1=\SI{8}{\mu m}$.

\textbf{Method: Doppler shift twice.} In the electron rest frame $\hbar\omega'\ll m_ec^2$ (hint), so the Compton shift is negligible and $\omega'_\text{out}\approx \omega'_\text{in} \equiv \omega'$.

Apply the relativistic Doppler formula lab $\to$ electron rest frame (head-on collision: photon along $-\hat{\bm x}$, electron along $+\hat{\bm x}$). Electron sees a blueshifted photon:
\[ \omega' = \gamma(1+\beta)\,\omega_1. \]
In the rest frame the photon backscatters: outgoing photon along $+\hat{\bm x}$. Apply Doppler rest $\to$ lab (source moves $+\hat{\bm x}$, observer at rest sees approaching source -- blueshift):
\[ \omega_2 = \gamma(1+\beta)\,\omega'. \]
Combine the two boosts:
\[ \omega_2 = \gamma^2(1+\beta)^2\,\omega_1. \]
Apply ultra-relativistic limit $\beta\to 1$:
\[ (1+\beta)^2 \to 4. \]
Hence:
\[ \omega_2 \approx 4\gamma^2\,\omega_1. \]
Use $\lambda \propto 1/\omega$:
\[ \boxed{\;\lambda_2 \approx \lambda_1/(4\gamma^2).\;} \]

\noindent\textbf{Numerical.} Compute $1-\beta^2$ for $\beta=0.999$:
\[ 1-\beta^2 = 1-0.998001 = 0.001999. \]
Take inverse square root:
\[ \gamma = 1/\sqrt{0.001999} = 22.366. \]
Compute $\gamma^2$:
\[ \gamma^2 = 500.24. \]
Multiply by 4:
\[ 4\gamma^2 = 2000.95. \]
Form $\lambda_2$:
\[ \lambda_2 = \SI{8}{\mu m}/2000.95. \]
Evaluate:
\[ \boxed{\;\lambda_2 \approx \SI{4.0}{nm}.\;} \]

\subsection*{(d) Plane EM wave: $A^\mu$, $\bm E$, $\bm B$}

\textbf{Problem.} Plane wave $\omega$, $\bm k=(k_x,0,0)$. Write a possible $A^\mu$, find $\bm E,\bm B$.

Choose a Lorenz-gauge null 4-potential with $\hat{\bm y}$ polarization:
\[ A^\mu = (0,\, 0,\, A_0\cos(k_x x - \omega t),\, 0). \]
Apply $\bm E = -\nabla\phi - \partial_t\bm A$ with $\phi=0$:
\[ \bm E = -\partial_t\bm A. \]
Differentiate $A_y$ in time:
\[ \partial_t A_y = A_0\omega\sin(k_x x-\omega t). \]
Form $E_y = -\partial_t A_y$:
\[ E_y = -A_0\omega\sin(k_x x-\omega t). \]
Apply vacuum dispersion $\omega = ck_x$:
\[ E_y = -A_0 c k_x \sin(k_x x-\omega t). \]
Apply $\bm B = \nabla\times\bm A$ with only $A_y(x,t)$ nonzero:
\[ B_z = \partial_x A_y. \]
Differentiate:
\[ B_z = -A_0 k_x \sin(k_x x-\omega t). \]
Collect:
\[ \boxed{\;\bm E = -A_0 c k_x \sin(k_x x-\omega t)\hat{\bm y},\quad \bm B = -A_0 k_x \sin(k_x x-\omega t)\hat{\bm z}.\;} \]
Check $|\bm E|=c|\bm B|$:
\[ |\bm E|/|\bm B| = c. \]
Check the Poynting direction:
\[ \bm E\times\bm B = E_y B_z\,(\hat{\bm y}\times\hat{\bm z}) = A_0^2 c k_x^2\sin^2(k_x x-\omega t)\,\hat{\bm x}. \]
Transverse $\bm E,\bm B$ with $\bm E\times\bm B \parallel +\hat{\bm x}$ as required.

\subsection*{(e) Periodic-field undulator: lab vs.\ electron rest frame}

\textbf{Problem.} Undulator $A^\mu = (\phi/c,\bm 0)$, $\phi=\phi_0\cos(k_u z)$. Electron with $\bm v=(0,0,v_z)$, ultra-relativistic. Find $\bm E,\bm B$ in lab and rest frame; is the field in the rest frame an EM wave?

\textbf{Lab frame.} With $A^0 = \phi/c$, $\bm A = \bm 0$:
\[ \bm E = -\nabla\phi - \partial_t\bm A. \]
Drop $\partial_t\bm A$:
\[ \bm E = -\nabla\phi. \]
Differentiate $\phi = \phi_0\cos(k_u z)$:
\[ \nabla\phi = -\phi_0 k_u\sin(k_u z)\,\hat{\bm z}. \]
Negate:
\[ \bm E = \phi_0 k_u\sin(k_u z)\,\hat{\bm z}. \]
With $\bm A = \bm 0$:
\[ \bm B = \nabla\times\bm A = \bm 0. \]
\[ \boxed{\;\bm E_\text{lab} = \phi_0 k_u\sin(k_u z)\,\hat{\bm z},\quad \bm B_\text{lab}=\bm 0.\;} \]
A purely electrostatic, longitudinal, $z$-periodic field.

\textbf{Electron rest frame.} Boost along $+\hat{\bm z}$ with velocity $v$. Coordinate inverse transform:
\[ z = \gamma(z'+vt'),\qquad t = \gamma(t'+vz'/c^2). \]
Apply the field transformation rule; $\bm v\parallel\bm E$, so the parallel component is unchanged:
\[ E'_z = E_z. \]
Compute the perpendicular fields ($\bm E_\perp=0$ and $\bm B=0$):
\[ \bm E'_\perp = \gamma(\bm E_\perp + \bm v\times\bm B)_\perp = 0. \]
\[ \bm B'_\perp = \gamma(\bm B_\perp - \bm v\times\bm E/c^2)_\perp. \]
Apply $\bm v\times\bm E = 0$ (since $\bm v\parallel\bm E$):
\[ \bm B'_\perp = 0. \]
Substitute $z = \gamma(z'+vt')$ into the spatial argument:
\[ k_u z = \gamma k_u z' + \gamma k_u v\, t'. \]
Identify $k'_u = \gamma k_u$ and $\omega' = \gamma k_u v$:
\[ E'_z(\bm r',t') = \phi_0 k_u\sin(\gamma k_u z' + \gamma k_u v\,t'). \]
\[ \boxed{\;\bm E' = \phi_0 k_u \sin(\gamma k_u z' + \gamma k_u v\,t')\,\hat{\bm z}',\quad \bm B'=\bm 0.\;} \]
In the ultra-relativistic limit $v\to c$: $\omega'\approx \gamma k_u c$ and $\omega'/k'_u = v\approx c$.

\noindent\textbf{Is this an EM wave?} A vacuum plane EM wave satisfies $|\bm E|=c|\bm B|$ with $\bm E\perp\bm k$ and $\bm B\perp\bm k$, $\bm B\neq 0$. Here $\bm B'=0$ and $\bm E'\parallel\hat{\bm z}'$ (longitudinal to the propagation direction $\hat{\bm z}'$).
\[ \boxed{\;\text{Not an EM wave: longitudinal }\bm E,\ \bm B'=0.\;} \]
It is a longitudinal oscillating electric field --- the rest-frame view of a static periodic potential as seen by a co-moving observer (no radiation; not transverse).

\section*{Question 4 --- Polar/axial vectors, $F^{\mu\nu}$, capacitor}

\subsection*{(a) Polar vs.\ axial; $\bm E,\bm B$}

\textbf{Problem.} Define polar/axial 3-vectors. Are $\bm E,\bm B$ polar or axial? Justify via Lorentz force.

\textbf{Polar vector:} flips sign under spatial inversion $\bm r\to-\bm r$ (e.g.\ $\bm r,\bm v,\bm p,\bm E$).

\textbf{Axial (pseudo-)vector:} unchanged under inversion (e.g.\ $\bm L=\bm r\times\bm p$, $\bm B$, $\bm\omega$).

Write the Lorentz force:
\[ \bm F = q(\bm E + \bm v\times\bm B). \]
Apply parity $\bm r\to -\bm r$, $\bm v\to -\bm v$, scalar $q$ unchanged. Since $\bm F\propto\dv*{\bm p}{t}$ is polar:
\[ \bm F \to -\bm F. \]
Write the transformed equation in primed fields:
\[ -\bm F = q\bm E' + q(-\bm v)\times\bm B'. \]
Set equal to $-\bm F = -q\bm E - q\bm v\times\bm B$:
\[ -q\bm E - q\bm v\times\bm B = q\bm E' - q\bm v\times\bm B'. \]
Match the $\bm v$-independent term:
\[ \bm E' = -\bm E. \]
Match the $\bm v$-dependent term:
\[ -q\bm v\times\bm B = -q\bm v\times\bm B'. \]
Solve:
\[ \bm B' = \bm B. \]
Hence $\bm E$ flips sign (polar) and $\bm B$ does not (axial).
\[ \boxed{\;\bm E\text{ polar},\ \bm B\text{ axial}.\;} \]

\subsection*{(b) 4-current, continuity, Lorentz force on a charge density}

\textbf{Problem.} Define $J^\mu$, write continuity, derive $f_\mu = J^\nu F_{\nu\mu}$, identify $f_0$.

Define 4-current:
\[ J^\mu = \rho_0 U^\mu = (\rho c, \bm j),\qquad \bm j = \rho\bm v. \]
Write continuity:
\[ \boxed{\;\partial_\mu J^\mu = 0 \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad \partial_t\rho + \nabla\!\cdot\!\bm j = 0.\;} \]

\noindent\textbf{Derive $f_\mu = J^\nu F_{\nu\mu}$.} Write the field tensor (covariant form):
\[ F_{\nu\mu} = \partial_\nu A_\mu - \partial_\mu A_\nu. \]
List components in signature $(+,-,-,-)$:
\[ F_{0i} = E_i/c,\qquad F_{ij} = -\epsilon_{ijk}B_k. \]
Take $\mu = i$ in $J^\nu F_{\nu i}$:
\[ J^\nu F_{\nu i} = J^0 F_{0 i} + J^j F_{j i}. \]
Apply $F_{ji} = -F_{ij} = \epsilon_{ijk}B_k$:
\[ J^\nu F_{\nu i} = (\rho c)(E_i/c) + j_j\epsilon_{ijk}B_k. \]
Recognise $\epsilon_{ijk}j_j B_k = (\bm j\times\bm B)_i$:
\[ J^\nu F_{\nu i} = \rho E_i + (\bm j\times\bm B)_i. \]
Identify $f_i = \rho E_i + (\bm j\times\bm B)_i$ as the Lorentz force density:
\[ J^\nu F_{\nu i} = f_i. \]

\noindent\textbf{$f_0$ component:}
Take $\mu = 0$:
\[ J^\nu F_{\nu 0} = J^0 F_{00} + J^j F_{j0}. \]
Apply $F_{00}=0$ and $F_{j0} = -F_{0j} = -E_j/c$:
\[ J^\nu F_{\nu 0} = -j^j E_j/c. \]
Identify $j^j E_j = \bm j\cdot\bm E$:
\[ |f_0| = \bm j\!\cdot\!\bm E/c. \]
Interpret: $|f_0|\cdot c = \bm j\cdot\bm E$ is the power per unit volume delivered by the field to the charges.
\[ \boxed{\;|f_0|\,c = \bm j\!\cdot\!\bm E = \text{(power per unit volume delivered by field to charges)}.\;} \]

\subsection*{(c) $F^{\alpha\beta}$ from given $A^\mu$}

\textbf{Problem.} $A^\mu = (0,A_0\cos(K\!\cdot\!X), A_0\sin(K\!\cdot\!X),0)$, $K=(\omega/c,0,0,k_z)$. Compute $F^{\alpha\beta}=\partial^\alpha A^\beta-\partial^\beta A^\alpha$.

Define the phase:
\[ \Phi \equiv K^\mu X_\mu = (\omega/c)(ct) + k_z(-z) = \omega t - k_z z. \]
List components: $A^1=A_0\cos\Phi$, $A^2=A_0\sin\Phi$, $A^0=A^3=0$.

Differentiate the phase:
\[ \partial_0\Phi = \omega/c,\qquad \partial_3\Phi = -k_z. \]
Apply $\partial^0 = \partial_0$ and $\partial^i = -\partial_i$.

Compute $\partial^0 A^1$:
\[ \partial^0 A^1 = (1/c)\partial_t (A_0\cos\Phi) = -A_0(\omega/c)\sin\Phi. \]
Compute $\partial^0 A^2$:
\[ \partial^0 A^2 = (1/c)\partial_t (A_0\sin\Phi) = A_0(\omega/c)\cos\Phi. \]
Compute $\partial^3 A^1$:
\[ \partial^3 A^1 = -\partial_z(A_0\cos\Phi) = -A_0\sin\Phi\cdot k_z = -A_0 k_z\sin\Phi. \]
Compute $\partial^3 A^2$:
\[ \partial^3 A^2 = -\partial_z(A_0\sin\Phi) = -A_0\cos\Phi\cdot(-k_z) = A_0 k_z\cos\Phi. \]
All other $\partial^\mu A^\nu$ vanish (no $x,y$ dependence).

Assemble $F^{\alpha\beta} = \partial^\alpha A^\beta - \partial^\beta A^\alpha$:
\[ F^{01} = \partial^0 A^1 - \partial^1 A^0 = -A_0(\omega/c)\sin\Phi. \]
\[ F^{02} = \partial^0 A^2 - \partial^2 A^0 = A_0(\omega/c)\cos\Phi. \]
\[ F^{31} = \partial^3 A^1 - \partial^1 A^3 = -A_0 k_z\sin\Phi. \]
\[ F^{32} = \partial^3 A^2 - \partial^2 A^3 = A_0 k_z\cos\Phi. \]
\[ F^{12} = \partial^1 A^2 - \partial^2 A^1 = 0. \]
Identify $\bm E$ via $F^{0i} = -E_i/c$:
\[ E_x = -cF^{01} = A_0\omega\sin\Phi. \]
\[ E_y = -cF^{02} = -A_0\omega\cos\Phi. \]
\[ E_z = 0. \]
Identify $\bm B$ via $F^{ij} = -\epsilon^{ijk}B_k$; so $F^{31} = -\epsilon^{312}B_2 = -B_y$ and $F^{32} = -\epsilon^{321}B_1 = +B_x$:
\[ B_x = F^{32} = A_0 k_z\cos\Phi. \]
\[ B_y = -F^{31} = A_0 k_z\sin\Phi. \]
\[ B_z = 0. \]
Collect:
\[ \bm E = (A_0\omega\sin\Phi,\ -A_0\omega\cos\Phi,\ 0). \]
\[ \bm B = (A_0 k_z\cos\Phi,\ A_0 k_z\sin\Phi,\ 0). \]
Compute magnitudes and orthogonality:
\[ |\bm E|=A_0\omega,\qquad |\bm B|=A_0 k_z,\qquad |\bm E|=c|\bm B|\ \text{(use $\omega = ck_z$)}. \]
\[ \bm E\!\cdot\!\bm B = A_0^2\omega k_z(\sin\Phi\cos\Phi - \cos\Phi\sin\Phi) = 0. \]
\[ \boxed{\;\bm E\perp\bm B,\ |\bm E|=c|\bm B|;\ \text{circularly polarised plane wave along }+\hat{\bm z}.\;} \]
Verify Poynting along $+\hat{\bm z}$:
\[ (\bm E\times\bm B)_z = E_x B_y - E_y B_x. \]
Substitute:
\[ (\bm E\times\bm B)_z = (A_0\omega\sin\Phi)(A_0 k_z\sin\Phi) - (-A_0\omega\cos\Phi)(A_0 k_z\cos\Phi). \]
Simplify:
\[ (\bm E\times\bm B)_z = A_0^2\omega k_z(\sin^2\Phi + \cos^2\Phi) = A_0^2\omega k_z. \]

\subsection*{(d) Field transformation under boost}

\textbf{Problem.} $S'$ moves with 3-velocity $\bm v$ relative to $S$. Write $\bm E'$, $\bm B'$.

Decompose into components parallel ($\parallel$) and perpendicular ($\perp$) to $\bm v$. Parallel components unchanged:
\[ \bm E'_\parallel = \bm E_\parallel,\qquad \bm B'_\parallel = \bm B_\parallel. \]
Perpendicular components transform with $\gamma$ and mixing:
\[ \bm E'_\perp = \gamma\bigl(\bm E_\perp + \bm v\times\bm B\bigr). \]
\[ \bm B'_\perp = \gamma\bigl(\bm B_\perp - (\bm v\times\bm E)/c^2\bigr). \]
\[ \boxed{\;\bm E' = \bm E_\parallel + \gamma(\bm E_\perp + \bm v\times\bm B),\quad \bm B' = \bm B_\parallel + \gamma\bigl(\bm B_\perp - (\bm v\times\bm E)/c^2\bigr).\;} \]

\subsection*{(e) Capacitor + magnetic field}

\textbf{Problem.} Plates parallel to $yz$-plane, gap $d=\SI{0.1}{m}$, $E=\SI{100}{MV/m}$ between plates, electron launched from negative plate at rest. (i) Energy and velocity at the second plate; (ii) can a $\bm B$ prevent it hitting positive plate; (iii) direction; (iv) magnitude; (v) trajectory sketch.

\textbf{(i) Energy and velocity at the second plate.}

Compute kinetic energy from work done by $\bm E$:
\[ E_\text{kin} = eEd. \]
Substitute $E = \SI{100}{MV/m}$, $d=\SI{0.1}{m}$:
\[ E_\text{kin} = e\cdot\SI{100}{MV/m}\cdot\SI{0.1}{m} = \SI{10}{MeV}. \]
Form the total energy:
\[ E_\text{tot} = m_e c^2 + E_\text{kin} = 0.511 + 10 = \SI{10.511}{MeV}. \]
Compute the Lorentz factor:
\[ \gamma = E_\text{tot}/(m_e c^2) = 10.511/0.511. \]
Evaluate:
\[ \gamma = 20.57. \]
Compute $1/\gamma^2$:
\[ 1/\gamma^2 = 1/423.1 = 0.002364. \]
Apply $\beta = \sqrt{1-1/\gamma^2}$:
\[ \beta = \sqrt{1-0.002364} = \sqrt{0.99764} = 0.99882. \]
\[ \boxed{\;E_\text{kin}\approx \SI{10}{MeV},\ \gamma\approx 20.6,\ v\approx 0.999\,c.\;} \]

\textbf{(ii) Can $\bm B$ prevent it reaching the positive plate?}

Yes. A magnetic field perpendicular to the electron velocity bends its trajectory; if the field is strong enough, the gyroradius is smaller than the gap so that the electron curves back to the negative plate before hitting the positive plate.
\[ \boxed{\;\text{Yes --- a transverse }\bm B\text{ bends the electron back.}\;} \]

\textbf{(iii) Direction of $\bm B$.} Take electron initial $\bm v$ along $+\hat{\bm x}$. To bend it inside the $xz$ (or $xy$) plane back toward the negative plate, $\bm B$ must lie in the $yz$-plane (perpendicular to $\bm v$). Choosing $\bm B = B\hat{\bm y}$:
\[ \boxed{\;\bm B \parallel \hat{\bm y}\text{ (or }\hat{\bm z}\text{): perpendicular to }\bm v\text{ and to }\bm E.\;} \]
The electron then executes (modified) cyclotron motion in the $xz$-plane.

\textbf{(iv) Strength of $\bm B$ to stop the electron at $x=d$.}

Energy is conserved by $\bm E$ alone ($\bm B$ does no work):
\[ \gamma(x)m_e c^2 = m_e c^2 + eEx. \]
Choose gauge $\bm A = -Bx\hat{\bm z}$ for $\bm B = B\hat{\bm y}$:
\[ \nabla\times\bm A = -(\partial_x A_z)\hat{\bm y} = B\hat{\bm y}\ \checkmark. \]
System has translation symmetry along $z$, so $z$-component of canonical momentum is conserved:
\[ \Pi_z = p_z + qA_z = \text{const},\qquad q=-e. \]
At launch $p_z=0$, $x=0\Rightarrow A_z=0$:
\[ \Pi_z = 0. \]
Substitute $A_z = -Bx$ and $q = -e$ at general $x$:
\[ p_z(x) + (-e)(-Bx) = 0. \]
Solve for $p_z(x)$:
\[ p_z(x) = -eBx. \]
At turn-around $v_x = 0$, so $p_x=0$ and $|p_z| = p_\text{tot}$:
\[ p_\text{tot}(x_\text{max}) = eBx_\text{max}. \]
Apply energy-momentum relation:
\[ (p_\text{tot}c)^2 = E_\text{tot}^2 - (m_ec^2)^2. \]
Set turn-around at $x_\text{max} = d$:
\[ eBd = p_\text{tot}(d). \]
Solve for $B$:
\[ B = p_\text{tot}(d)/(ed). \]
Compute $(p_\text{tot}c)^2$ at $E_\text{tot}=\SI{10.511}{MeV}$:
\[ (p_\text{tot}c)^2 = 10.511^2 - 0.511^2. \]
Evaluate:
\[ (p_\text{tot}c)^2 = 110.481 - 0.261 = 110.22\,(\text{MeV})^2. \]
Take the square root:
\[ p_\text{tot}c = \SI{10.499}{MeV}. \]
Form $B = (p_\text{tot}c)/(ec d)$:
\[ B = \frac{p_\text{tot}c}{ecd} = \frac{\SI{10.499e6}{V}}{c\,\SI{0.1}{m}}. \]
Insert $c = \SI{2.998e8}{m/s}$:
\[ B = \frac{\SI{10.499e6}{V}}{\SI{2.998e8}{m/s}\times\SI{0.1}{m}}. \]
Compute denominator:
\[ (\SI{2.998e8}{m/s})(\SI{0.1}{m}) = \SI{2.998e7}{m^2/s}. \]
Divide:
\[ B = \frac{\SI{10.499e6}{V}}{\SI{2.998e7}{m^2/s}} = 0.350\,\text{T}. \]
\[ \boxed{\;B \approx \SI{0.35}{T}.\;} \]

\textbf{(v) Trajectory sketch.}

The electron starts at the negative plate with $\bm v=\bm 0$. Initially $\bm B$ exerts no force; the $\bm E$-field accelerates it along $+\hat{\bm x}$. As $v_x$ grows the magnetic force becomes:
\[ \bm F_\text{mag} = -e\bm v\times\bm B = -e v_x\hat{\bm x}\times B\hat{\bm y} = -ev_xB\hat{\bm z}. \]
This deflects the electron toward $-\hat{\bm z}$. The trajectory curves; at the threshold $B = \SI{0.35}{T}$, $v_x\to 0$ exactly at $x=d$ (the electron just grazes the positive plate before turning back). For $B$ slightly above threshold, the electron turns back before reaching $x=d$ and executes a cycloidal drift in the $xz$-plane.
